## 1. Music similarity: which features capture "vibe", how to compute them live in a browser, how to make them robust, and which learned embeddings are usable

### Takeaway
A hand-built "vibe fingerprint" of roughly 70 numbers (timbre statistics, band contrast and flatness, key-relative CENS chroma with key clarity and mode, harmonic-change rate, onset rate, a compact fluctuation/rhythm profile, DFA danceability, dynamics, articulation and per-role activity), computed on loudness-normalised audio, is implementable in TypeScript under GPL from code that may be ported (Musly MPL-2.0, librosa ISC, rp_extract GPL-3.0) plus formulas from essentia and MIRtoolbox. Learned embeddings can add a semantic axis, but only CLAP (Apache-2.0 card, ONNX via transformers.js), OpenL3 (CC BY 4.0 weights) and EfficientAT (MIT) have licences compatible with a GPL app that may be sold; MERT and all MTG/Essentia models are non-commercial and are out.

### Cited Findings

**Classic content-based similarity: Musly (MPL-2.0 → PORT, keep MPL notice on ported files)**
- [SRC] Musly is "a program and library for high performance audio music similarity computation … only uses the audio signal"; "The source code is released under the MPL 2.0". — [musly README](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/README.md)
- [SRC] Default method `timbre`: 22 050 Hz, Hann window 1024, hop 50 %, only the **central 60 s** of a track (`start = (length − 60 s)/2`), 36 mel bands, **25 MFCCs**, one full-covariance Gaussian per track; distance = **Jensen–Shannon divergence** between Gaussians, then **Mutual Proximity** normalisation (citing Schnitzer et al., ISMIR 2011). — [timbre.cpp](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/libmusly/methods/timbre.cpp)
- [SRC] Method `mandelellis`: same front end, 20 MFCCs, symmetrised Kullback–Leibler divergence ("Mandel and Ellis … Song-level features and support vector machines for music classification, ISMIR 2005"). — [mandelellis.cpp](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/libmusly/methods/mandelellis.cpp)
- [SRC] Front end: triangular mel filters from 20 Hz to Nyquist with `mel = 1127.01048·ln(1 + f/700)`; MFCC = DCT-II of `log(1 + melPower)`; DCT rows scaled `1/sqrt(n/2)`, row 0 additionally × √2/2 (c0 is kept). — [melspectrum.cpp](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/libmusly/melspectrum.cpp), [mfcc.cpp](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/libmusly/mfcc.cpp), [discretecosinetransform.cpp](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/libmusly/discretecosinetransform.cpp)
- [SRC] Gaussian: sample mean, covariance divided by N − 1, `covar.diagonal() += 1e-4` "to avoid singular covariance matrices in case the input data was silence"; stores the upper triangle and log|Σ|. Jensen–Shannon as coded: merged Gaussian `Σm = ½(Σ0 + Σ1) + δδᵀ`, `δ = ½(μ0 − μ1)`; `jsd = −¼(log|Σ0| + log|Σ1|) + ½ log|Σm|` (½ log|Σm| via the sum of logs of the Cholesky diagonal); returns `sqrt(max(0, jsd))`. — [gaussianstatistics.cpp](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/libmusly/gaussianstatistics.cpp)
- [SRC] Mutual Proximity: for every track keep mean μ and standard deviation σ of its distances to a reference set; normalised distance between seed s and track x = `1 − (1 − Φ((d − μs)/σs))·(1 − Φ((d − μx)/σx))`, Φ by Abramowitz–Stegun 7.1.26. It turns a raw distance into "how unusually close are these two for both of them", which suppresses "hub" tracks that are near everything. — [mutualproximity.cpp](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/libmusly/mutualproximity.cpp)
- [CALC] A 25-coefficient Gaussian is 25 means + 325 covariance terms + 1 log-determinant = 351 floats (1.4 kB). Live cost: one 1024-point FFT per 512 samples at 22.05 kHz ≈ 43 frames/s; mean and covariance can be updated incrementally (Welford) per frame in a Worker.

**Rhythm and "fluctuation patterns": rp_extract (GPL-3.0 → PORT; the ported files carry GPL-3.0 as published) and the MA toolbox**
- [SRC] Rhythm Patterns (also called Fluctuation Patterns) per ≈ 6 s segment ("segment_size should always be ~6 sec, fft_window_size should always be ~ 23ms": 2^16 samples at 11 kHz, 2^17 at 22 kHz, 2^18 at 44 kHz); 24 Bark bands with edges 100 … 15 500 Hz; spectral-masking spread `10^((15.81 + 7.5(Δ + 0.474) − 17.5·sqrt(1 + (Δ + 0.474)²))/10)`; dB → phon (equal-loudness table) → sone; an FFT of each band's loudness envelope over the segment; modulation resolution `1/(segment_size/sr)` ≈ 0.17 Hz; 60 modulation bins kept (≈ 0–10 Hz); fluctuation-strength weighting `1/(f_mod/4 + 4/f_mod)` (maximum at 4 Hz); song value = median over segments, lead-in and fade-out segments skipped; also a 60-value Rhythm Histogram (patterns summed over bands) and a Statistical Spectrum Descriptor (per band mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis, median, min, max). — [rp_extract.py](https://github.com/tuwien-musicir/rp_extract/blob/master/rp_extract.py), [LICENSE](https://github.com/tuwien-musicir/rp_extract/blob/master/LICENSE)
- [SEC] The MA toolbox's Fluctuation Patterns use a fluctuation model peaking at 4 Hz (240 bpm) and Euclidean distance. — [ISMIR 2004 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2004/paper/000180.pdf) (not opened; search summary)

**High-level descriptors: essentia (AGPL-3.0 → FORMULA only), read at commit 23ae7ba**
- [SRC] **Danceability** (detrended fluctuation analysis, Streich & Herrera 2005): 10 ms frames; s(n) = standard deviation of the samples in each frame; subtract the mean and integrate; for τ from 310 ms to 8 800 ms in steps × 1.1, slide blocks of τ (jump `max(τ/50, 1)`), F(τ) = sqrt(mean residual of a straight-line fit per block); exponents `α_i = log10(F(τ_{i+1})/F(τ_i)) / log10((τ_{i+1} + 3)/(τ_i + 3))`; **danceability = 1/mean(α)**, "usually range from 0 to 3 (higher values meaning more danceable)". — [danceability.cpp](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/highlevel/danceability.cpp), [danceability.h](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/highlevel/danceability.h)
- [SRC] **Dynamic complexity** (Streich 2007): "average absolute deviation from the global loudness level estimate on the dB scale"; 0.2 s frames; "cheap B-curve loudness compensation" (one-pole high-pass b = {0.98595, −0.98595}, a = {1, −0.9719}); energy smoothed with a 35 ms constant; leading and trailing silence removed; global loudness = dB values weighted by `0.9^(−dB)` (loud frames dominate); complexity = mean |dB − loudness|; a half-silent, half-full-scale signal would score 45, so "a safe normalization would be using 45". — [dynamiccomplexity.cpp](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/highlevel/dynamiccomplexity.cpp)
- [SRC] **Intensity** (relaxed/moderate/aggressive from spectral complexity, roll-off, peaks and kurtosis) is labelled by its own documentation "Quality: outdated (non-reliable, poor accuracy)". — [intensity.cpp](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/highlevel/intensity.cpp)
- [SRC] **Spectral contrast** (octave-based, modified for robustness): defaults frame 2048, 22 050 Hz, 6 bands, 20–11 000 Hz, `neighbourRatio` 0.4 (share of a band's bins averaged for its peak and its valley), `staticDistribution` 0.15 (share of bins spread evenly, the rest logarithmically); contrast per band `−(peak/valley)^(1/ln(bandMean))`, valley output `ln(valley)`. — [spectralcontrast.cpp](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/spectral/spectralcontrast.cpp), [spectralcontrast.h](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/spectral/spectralcontrast.h)
- [SRC] **Effective duration**: time the envelope stays above `thresholdRatio` = 0.4 of its maximum (default). — [effectiveduration.h](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/temporal/effectiveduration.h)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) MIRtoolbox's `miremotion` regression (GPL-2.0-only → FORMULA): activity from RMS, fluctuation peak, centroid, spread, spectral entropy; valence from RMS variability, fluctuation peak, key clarity, mode, spectral novelty; tension from harmonic change, key clarity, chroma novelty. — [miremotion.m](https://github.com/olivierlar/mirtoolbox/blob/71d6265/MIRToolbox/%40miremotion/miremotion.m)
- [SEC] Published definitions of a streaming service's descriptors (endpoint marked deprecated; no formulas published): energy "a perceptual measure of intensity and activity … energetic tracks feel fast, loud, and noisy"; danceability "based on a combination of musical elements including tempo, rhythm stability, beat strength, and overall regularity"; valence "musical positiveness"; acousticness "a confidence measure … of whether the track is acoustic"; instrumentalness above 0.5 means instrumental, and "ooh" and "aah" count as instrumental. — [AudioFeatures interface docs](https://spotify.js.org/interfaces/AudioFeatures.html), [developer console page](https://developer.spotify.com/console/get-audio-features-track/) (blocked here)

**Robust harmony features: librosa CENS (ISC → PORT)**
- [SRC] `chroma_cens`: L1-normalise each chroma vector; quantise with thresholds [0.4, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05], each adding 0.25 ("log-like" amplitude steps); smooth over time with a normalised Hann window of 41 (+2) frames; L2-normalise. **Robustness to loudness and production [CALC unless marked]**
- Ratio features are invariant to a global gain: spectral centroid, flatness, crest, contrast (`peak/valley`), CENS (normalised), key correlations, onset rate, tempo, DFA exponents (scale-free by construction). Absolute levels, log-mel energies and MFCC c0 move with gain.
- But they are not invariant to equalisation: on a pink-like spectrum (20 Hz–11 kHz), a +3 dB shelf above 5 kHz moved the magnitude-weighted centroid from 3 833 Hz to 4 324 Hz (+12.8 %) and full-band flatness from 0.421 to 0.547 (+30 %). Hence measure flatness and contrast **per band** (4–6 bands), express centroid in octaves, and give "brightness" a modest weight in the fingerprint.
- Musly keeps c0 and takes `log(1 + P)` [SRC above]; for a fingerprint that should ignore mastering loudness, drop c0, start from loudness-normalised audio (integrated or long short-term K-weighted loudness, earlier notes), and use the covariance or log-variances of c1–c12 so that texture, not level, dominates.
- Mastering compression changes dynamics descriptors (crest, loudness range, dynamic complexity) for the same arrangement; treat dynamics as a small-weight group.
- Hub suppression: Mutual Proximity [SRC above] or simply rank-normalising distances to a reference set keeps a few "average-sounding" songs from looking like everything.

### Inferences
- [CALC] **Proposed vibe fingerprint (≈ 74 numbers, ≈ 300 bytes)**, all computed in a Worker fed by the AudioWorklet, on audio first normalised by a running K-weighted loudness (earlier notes):
  - **Timbre (35):** MFCC c1–c12 means (12) and log-variances (12) over a 20–30 s window (or the full 12×12 covariance for a Jensen–Shannon timbre distance, as Musly); spectral contrast, 6 bands (6); flatness in 4 bands (4); centroid in octaves re 1 kHz (1).
  - **Harmony (16):** key clarity (1), majorness = best major correlation − best minor correlation (1), harmonic-change rate (tonnetz distance per second) (1), roughness/dissonance (1), **CENS chroma rotated to the detected tonic** (12) so transposed songs compare as similar.
  - **Rhythm (10):** log2(tempo/120) folded into one octave (1), onset rate per second (1), pulse clarity = height of the main onset-autocorrelation peak (1), DFA danceability (1), a rhythm histogram compressed to 6 log-spaced modulation bands from 0.5 to 10 Hz with the 4 Hz weighting (6).
  - **Dynamics and articulation (5):** loudness range (10th–95th percentile of short-term loudness) (1), dynamic complexity (1), crest (1), share of short versus held events from the existing short/held decision (1), median effective duration / inter-onset interval ("legato ratio") (1).
  - **Texture and roles (8):** harmonic-to-percussive energy ratio from median-filter HPSS (1), mean number of active layers (1), presence shares of drums, bass, chords, melody, vocals from stems or tracks (5), noisiness (1).
- [CALC] **Normalise across songs, not within one:** z-score each dimension against fixed reference statistics computed once offline over a reference set (for example the app's demo songs plus a public set) and shipped as constants, then squash with `tanh(z/2)`; per-song z-scoring would erase exactly the differences that should make two songs look different. Compare with a weighted Euclidean distance in which each of the five groups has equal total weight, and optionally Jensen–Shannon for the timbre Gaussian.
- [CALC] **Time scales:** a *song vibe* (whole-file, available instantly for a decoded file, or converging over the first 20–30 s live), a *section vibe* (the app knows section boundaries from its Frame page; for outside audio use a novelty curve), and the *moment* (the existing per-event features). The song vibe sets palette family, layout family and mark families; the section vibe drifts within them; the moment sets each mark.
- [CALC] **For the app's own songs** there is a cheaper, exact path: tempo, key, mode, chord changes per bar, note density and articulation per track can be read from the arrangement itself, so the fingerprint can be computed symbolically and the audio features used only for timbre and production. For outside songs, the stems pipeline (sibling notes `stems.md`) supplies per-role audio.
- [CALC] **Testable robustness target:** the fingerprint of a song should move less between "same mix at −6 dB", "same mix with ±3 dB shelves", "MP3 at 128 kbit/s" than between two different songs of the same style. These are cheap unit tests with rendered audio.
- [CALC] Embeddings: an EfficientAT tagger (≈ 1–5 M parameters) or the CLAP audio tower via transformers.js could add 8–16 semantic dimensions (e.g. CLAP similarity to text prompts such as "calm", "aggressive", "acoustic", "electronic", "sparse", "dense"), giving descriptors like "acousticness" that hand features do poorly. Keep it optional, off the audio thread, and run it once per 5–10 s window; the hand features alone already satisfy "similar elements → similar painting" and are fully explainable.

## 2. Deterministic, continuous mappings from the fingerprint to palette, composition, mark families, motion and density; seeding randomness

### Takeaway
Split the mapping into two kinds of function of the fingerprint: smooth (Lipschitz) functions for every continuous visual parameter, and "feature-space value noise" for the arbitrary-but-stable choices, i.e. random numbers attached to the corners of a coarse lattice over a 3–4-dimensional projection of the fingerprint and blended smoothly between corners. Discrete choices (layout, mark family) become soft scores with hysteresis. Fine detail stays random through per-mark seeds from note identity plus a visible run number. Similar songs then land close together in every parameter, songs from different regions differ, and the same file always repaints identically.

### Cited Findings
- [SRC] (earlier notes) "All randomness flows from the seed. Same seed + params = identical output. Never call Math.random() or read the clock in the art path"; judge on a contact sheet of at least 9 seeds. — [algorithmic-art-skill SKILL.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) mulberry32 seeded from an FNV-style string hash with `fork(salt)` sub-streams and an order-independent coordinate hash `n2(x, y)`. — [generative-painting-skills rng.js](https://github.com/debuu0007/generative-painting-skills)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) Seeded value, gaussian and 1D–4D simplex noise (MIT). — [canvas-sketch-util random.md](https://github.com/mattdesl/canvas-sketch-util)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) Per-element reseeding by a stable key so one change does not reshuffle the rest; lattice placement where every property is a pure function of the cell's integer coordinates. — [ClaudeAnimationBase](https://github.com/JohnHeibel/ClaudeAnimationBase); [Unstill-Life](https://github.com/RichardPieters/Unstill-Life)
- [SRC] The app already seeds by section letter: `Spot.key` "the section's letter: the same letter is the same painting", `index` "shifts its colours". — `src/canvas/layout.ts` (working tree)
- [SRC] Mutual Proximity re-scales distances per track (section 1) — the same idea can rescale fingerprint distances before they drive visuals. — [mutualproximity.cpp](https://github.com/dominikschnitzer/musly/blob/master/libmusly/mutualproximity.cpp)
- [SEC, memory] Random-hyperplane locality-sensitive hashing (Charikar 2002): for a random hyperplane through the origin, two vectors get the same sign bit with probability 1 − θ/π, where θ is the angle between them, so nearby vectors share most bits of a k-bit hash. Not opened this session.
- [SEC, memory] Blockchain generative-art platforms derive every trait from one token hash through a seeded PRNG; traits are deliberately uncorrelated with any input, which is the opposite of what is needed here. Not opened this session.
- [SRC] (earlier notes) Mood colour practice: arousal-weighted saturation and warm/cool arcs, palette held 95 % to a slow value at high arousal; build palettes in a perceptual space, 3–5 colours, accent under ~15 % of pixels. — [music-reactive-lighting](https://github.com/CanYuzbey/music-reactive-lighting); [algorithmic-art-skill palettes.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill)

### Inferences
- [CALC] **Step 1, interpretable axes.** Project the 74-number fingerprint onto 4 axes with fixed weights: *energy* (loudness range inverted, onset rate, flatness, centroid, percussive ratio), *brightness/valence* (majorness, key clarity, centroid, low roughness), *groove* (danceability, pulse clarity, 2–4 Hz rhythm bands), *density/texture* (active layers, spectral contrast inverted, harmonic-change rate). `a_k = tanh(Σ_j w_kj·z_j)` ∈ (−1, 1). Hand weights keep it explainable; MIRtoolbox's regression signs (earlier notes) are a starting point for energy and valence.
- [CALC] **Step 2, continuous parameters** are smooth functions of `a` (and of the full fingerprint where useful), e.g. in OKLCH: ground lightness `L0 = 0.93 − 0.10·energy01`; palette chroma `C = 0.04 + 0.12·energy01`; palette centre hue `h = mix(235°, 55°, valence01)` with sweep width `30° + 120°·density01`; stroke speed and field turbulence ∝ energy; mean stroke length ∝ legato ratio; wetness/dilution ∝ (1 − energy01)·(legato) as an optional "manner" (the calm-is-wet link has no research behind it, earlier report). Because `tanh` and these mixes are Lipschitz, a small fingerprint change gives a proportionally small visual change.
- [CALC] **Step 3, feature-space value noise for "arbitrary" choices** (palette secondary hues, field seed, composition anchor points, brush-family variant): take 3 of the axes, divide by a cell size of 0.5 (≈ 4 cells per axis over (−1, 1)), and for each of the 2³ = 8 surrounding lattice corners `c` draw `r_c = hash(c, channel, run)` ∈ [0, 1); the parameter is `Σ_c w_c·r_c` with smoothstep (or quintic) multilinear weights. The result is continuous everywhere, identical for identical inputs, nearly identical for neighbours inside one cell, and different across distant cells. Seeded simplex noise evaluated at `(a1, a2, a3)` (canvas-sketch-util) is a drop-in alternative.
- [CALC] **Step 4, discrete choices** (layout family, mark family per role): score each option as `affinity(a) + 0.15·valueNoise(a, option)`; choose the arg-max, but for a live stream switch only when another option leads by a margin (hysteresis, e.g. 0.1) for at least one bar, and never mid-phrase. Within one song, fix the song-level choice after the first 20–30 s (or immediately for a decoded file) and let only section-level parameters drift.
- [CALC] **Step 5, seeds that are stable yet input-correlated:** `songSeed = hash(quantised a at cell size 0.5, run)` drives slow, structural randomness; to avoid a jump at a cell edge, blend structural parameters across the corner seeds as in step 3 rather than switching seeds. A random-hyperplane hash of the fingerprint (k ≈ 8–12 bits) is a simpler alternative that groups similar songs, at the cost of hard edges between buckets.
- [CALC] **Step 6, fine detail:** every mark's own randomness from `hash(layer, onset time rounded to 10 ms, pitch or band, run)` (as in the earlier notes), so two similar songs share style and structure while their marks, which follow their own notes, never repeat.
- [CALC] **Checks to automate:** (1) same audio twice → bit-identical parameter set; (2) for pairs of fingerprints, `|Δparams| ≤ L·|Δfingerprint|` with a fixed L (property test); (3) a contact sheet ordered by fingerprint distance should look like a gradual series.

### Gaps
- No open project was found that maps a music feature vector continuously to generative-art parameters with an explicit continuity guarantee; the value-noise-over-feature-space scheme is a proposal built from standard noise and hashing tools.
- The LSH and generative-platform statements are from memory, not opened sources.
- No study tests whether viewers judge "similar music → similar painting" by palette, by composition or by mark type; the relative weights are untested.

## 3. Per-layer visual vocabularies: role → mark, and legibility when layers overlap

### Takeaway
Give each role its own mark family that differs from the others on several redundant channels at once (geometry, edge hardness, scale, opacity, motion and depth order), keep hue as the weakest separator within one palette, and fix a drawing order (fog and washes underneath, fields and lines in the middle, impacts and glaze on top). The research supports a few links (pitch to height, lightness and size; harsh timbre to angular shape; bright timbre to lighter, more saturated colour); the specific role-to-mark assignments are design choices, and should be described as such.

### Cited Findings
- [SEC] Timbre ↔ shape: 23 musical sounds varying in pitch and timbre at fixed loudness; "strong association between timbre of envelope normalized sounds and visual shapes"; in that paradigm "fundamental frequency was not associated with height, grayscale or color". — [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00352/full) (blocked; search summary)
- [SEC] Timbre ↔ colour: semantic timbre ratings (bright, dark, warm …) "predicted the lightness and saturation of colors matched to instrument timbres". — [Frontiers in Psychology 2024/2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1520131/full) (search summary)
- [SEC] Texture correspondences: visual texture contrast ↔ sound brightness; texture periodicity ↔ auditory dissonance; coarseness/granularity ↔ sound compactness. — [QMUL crossmodal project page](https://comma.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/projects/crossmodal/) (search summary)
- [SEC] (earlier report) Pitch → height (B), pitch → lightness (A), pitch → smaller size (B), loudness → size/width (A), harsh → angular (A for shape), hue has no reliable acoustic partner (D). — [drawing study](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00789/full); [pitch–lightness study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6407832/)
- [SRC] Harmony procedural drawing tool (GPL-3.0-or-later → PORT): brushes `sketchy`, `shaded`, `chrome`, `fur`, `longfur`, `web`, `squares`, `ribbon`, `circles`, `grid`, `simple`. **Ribbon**: 50 "painters", each a damped spring chasing the pointer, `ax = (ax + (x − target)·div)·ease`, `div` 0.1, `ease` random 0.6–0.8, line alpha 0.05 × pressure, updated 60 times a second — a multi-strand ribbon that lags and overshoots. **Sketchy**: each new point links to earlier points within d² < 4000 px² with probability 1 − d²/2000, drawing only the middle 40 % of each link, alpha 0.05 — hatching that thickens where a path revisits itself. **Web**: d² < 2500, probability 0.1, alpha 0.1. **Shaded**: d² < 1000, alpha (1 − d²/1000)·0.1. — [mrdoob/harmony](https://github.com/mrdoob/harmony) (`js/brushes/*.js`, LICENSE "version 3 … or (at your option) any later version")
- [SRC] (earlier notes) Light should be added in a separate additive pass, because pigment-like mixing turns "a yellow glow painted over blue … green". — [ClaudeAnimationBase ANIMATION_GUIDE.md](https://github.com/JohnHeibel/ClaudeAnimationBase)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) A kick envelope below 150 Hz separate from a 40-band flux; p5.brush flow-field presets and pressure bells; ofxOilPaint bristles; watercolour layering at 4–5 % opacity. — [paint-mv-skills](https://github.com/lintsinghua/paint-mv-skills); [p5.brush](https://github.com/acamposuribe/p5.brush); [ofxOilPaint](https://github.com/jagracar/ofxOilPaint); [sighack/watercolor-simulation](https://github.com/sighack/watercolor-simulation)
- [SRC] The app's coverage guide currently knows three roles (`chords`, `melody`, `drums`). — `src/canvas/cover.ts` (working tree)

### Inferences
- [CALC] **Mark vocabulary per role** (all heights from frequency as now; all colours inside the song palette):

| Role | Mark family | Geometry and edge | Scale and opacity | What the sound changes | Code to start from |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kick / low drums | thump blot | round, soft-edged, slightly flattened, at the bottom | large, opacity 0.15–0.35 | level → size; attack sharpness → edge hardness; decay → halo length | dab stamper (existing), p5.brush fill bleed |

- [CALC] **Legibility rules:** (1) separate roles by spatial frequency: fog ≫ washes > bass band > melody line width > percussion specks, roughly a factor of 3 between neighbours, so an overlap reads as texture on top of field; (2) fixed depth order: fog → washes → bass → harmony → melody → vocal glaze (additive) → percussion on top; (3) at most two roles share a hue family; separate the others by value (light versus dark) before hue; (4) edges carry articulation (hard edge = sharp attack/staccato, lost edge = legato/pad); (5) the line roles (melody, harmony, vocal) never use the spatter family, and the percussion roles never draw long lines, so the role is readable from shape alone, even in greyscale.
- [CALC] **Similar elements → similar painting** follows if mark families are chosen per role and modulated by the role's own features: two songs that both have a busy hi-hat, a sustained pad and a stepwise melody will show the same stipple density at the top, the same fog and the same kind of line, whatever their key.
- [CALC] Role detection: for the app's own tracks the role is known (track type); for outside songs it comes from stem separation (sibling notes) with the bass and vocal stems mapped directly and "other" split by register and articulation (sustained wide-band → chords/pads; monophonic pitch track → melody).

### Gaps
- No study was found that maps instrument roles (drums, bass, chords, melody, vocals) to visual mark types, and none on the legibility of overlapping layered marks; all assignments above are design proposals.
- The timbre–shape and timbre–colour papers could not be opened; their effect sizes and the exact instruments used are unknown here.
- No open audio-painting project was found that separates more than three roles.

## 4. Composition layouts beyond a left-to-right sweep, with open code

### Takeaway
Keep one fixed spatial law per layout (frequency → height, or frequency → radius) and vary how time is spent: a scrolling river, bar-wise palimpsest, spiral/radial, centre-out blooms, flow-field carriers ("paint where the music is"), region-per-role, and a slow breathing zoom. Choose the family from the song fingerprint (groove → river or palimpsest or spiral; ambient → blooms or flow field; dense → region-per-role) and vary anchors per section, so compositions differ between songs but stay consistent for similar songs.

## 5. Coverage and fullness: paint in proportion to musical density

### Takeaway
Let the music set a target coverage, `C* = c_min + (c_max − c_min)·(1 − exp(−k·D))`, where D sums each active layer's normalised loudness; enforce it locally with a Hertzmann-style error controller and a per-layer paint ration per second, and keep 20–40 % of the ground bare. With c_min 0.04, c_max 0.78 and k 0.35, two quiet tracks aim for about 18 % coverage and six loud ones for about 67 %.

### Cited Findings
- [SRC] (earlier notes) An automatic oil painter stops adding where 0.65 of a stroke's path is already painted and rejects strokes that would destroy more than 0.4 of well-painted pixels; brushes from large to small (÷1.3 per pass). — [ofxOilSimulator.cpp](https://github.com/jagracar/ofxOilPaint)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) "Reserve 20–40% of the canvas as near-empty ground"; accent under ~15 % of pixels; tone-map dense accumulation with `log1p`. — [algorithmic-art-skill composition.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill)
- [SRC] Error-gated painting (paint only where target − canvas exceeds T), coarse to fine. — [PyPainterly painterly.py](https://github.com/pschaldenbrand/PyPainterly/blob/master/painterly.py)
- [SRC] The app's coverage guide averages per-band power over 6 s for three stems and reports level 0–1 relative to a 54 dB range. — `src/canvas/cover.ts` (working tree)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) An "even pace" transform (0.6) spreads bursty mark births into a steadier rate. — [Unstill-Life](https://github.com/RichardPieters/Unstill-Life)

### Inferences
- [CALC] **Musical density** `D(t) = Σ_roles a_i(t)·ℓ_i(t)`, with `a_i` ∈ {0, 1} (role active in the last bar: onsets or sustained energy above its gate) and `ℓ_i` ∈ [0, 1] the role's short-term loudness relative to the song's loud level (95th percentile), so a quiet song with two parts stays sparse and a dense loud chorus fills more.
- [CALC] Target coverage examples with `c_min = 0.04`, `c_max = 0.78`, `k = 0.35`: 1 quiet layer (D = 0.3) → 0.11; 2 quiet layers (D = 0.6) → 0.18; 2 loud layers (D = 1.8) → 0.39; 4 medium (D = 2.4) → 0.46; 6 loud (D = 5.4) → 0.67. `c_max` 0.78 keeps at least 22 % ground, inside the 20–40 % rule.
- [CALC] Opacity arithmetic for rations: n overlapping marks of opacity α reach `1 − (1 − α)^n`. At α = 0.04 reaching 20 % / 50 % / 70 % needs 5.5 / 17 / 29.5 layers; at α = 0.08, 2.7 / 8.3 / 14.4; at α = 0.15, 1.4 / 4.3 / 7.4. So washes (α 0.04–0.08) need many layers to fill, while impacts (α ≥ 0.15) fill quickly and need the strictest rations.
- [CALC] **Controller:** keep a low-resolution coverage map (e.g. 64 × 36 cells, opacity-weighted); a new mark is allowed in a cell only while `coverage_cell < C*·w_cell`, where `w_cell` is the layout's importance weight (more where the music "is"); otherwise shrink, thin or scumble it. Ration each role: `paint per second ≤ ρ_role·D_role`, so a dense hi-hat cannot cover the canvas alone.
- [CALC] **Fading for long songs:** fade old paint towards a floor (say 40 % of its contrast) with a half-life measured in bars or sections, not seconds (earlier notes: 0.992 per frame halves in ~1.4 s, far too fast for a painting); count faded paint at reduced weight in the coverage map so new sections get room without erasing history.
- [CALC] Quiet passages after loud ones: since `C*` falls, the controller simply stops adding; nothing has to be removed, and the ground already left bare stays visible.

### Gaps
- No source describes coverage budgeting for music paintings; the curve, the constants and the controller are proposals to tune by eye.
- How viewers perceive "fullness" (pixel coverage versus value contrast versus mark count) was not researched.

## 6. Responsiveness: audio-visual sync tolerances and per-layer features for attack, legato/staccato, roughness, brightness and intensity

### Takeaway
Paint that appears after its sound is the strict direction: broadcast standards detect audio-leading offsets from about 45 ms (acceptable to about 90 ms), while visuals may lead by up to about 125 ms before detection, and perceptual research finds the binding window narrower when audio leads. So use look-ahead (pre-analysis of decoded files, or a delay on the audible path) to land marks 0–20 ms *before* or exactly at the sound, and keep the live-input path under ~45 ms end to end. Per layer, attack comes from the onset slope or rise time, legato/staccato from sounding duration relative to inter-onset interval, roughness from the dissonance formulas, brightness from centroid and contrast, intensity from loudness relative to the song.

### Cited Findings
- [SEC] ITU-R BT.1359-1 (1998): detectability window +45 ms (audio leads) to −125 ms (audio lags); acceptability +90 ms to −185 ms (one source −190 ms); an NTIA report states the same thresholds with the opposite sign convention. — [Audio-to-video synchronization (overview)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio-to-video_synchronization); [NTIA report](https://its.ntia.gov/publications/download/11-474.pdf); [TV Technology](https://tvtechnology.com/opinions/av-synchronization-how-bad-is-bad)
- [SEC] Studies report detection thresholds for A/V desynchronisation from 45 ms to 250 ms; one cited study found detection at 35 ms audio lead or more than 100 ms video lead. — [arXiv 2212.01686](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.01686); [TV Technology](https://tvtechnology.com/opinions/av-synchronization-how-bad-is-bad)
- [SEC] The audiovisual temporal binding window spans "several hundred milliseconds" and is asymmetric: "narrower and non-malleable for auditory-leading stimulus pairs and wider and trainable for visual-leading pairs"; ~160–250 ms for flash–beep pairs; one study measured ~185 ms full bandwidth. — [Oxford publication record](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publications/502695); [PMC4958503](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4958503); [PMC6063927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6063927) (search summaries)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) Use `getOutputTimestamp()` (or `currentTime − outputLatency`) to map analysis time to audible time; analysis has a free look-ahead of `baseLatency + outputLatency`; onset detection is 15–30 ms late; short/held decision takes 40–100 ms; a `DelayNode` on the audible path buys look-ahead for file playback. — [Web Audio spec index.bs](https://github.com/WebAudio/web-audio-api/blob/42e974a/index.bs)
- [SRC] Effective duration = time above 0.4 of the envelope maximum (essentia default). — [effectiveduration.h](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/temporal/effectiveduration.h)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) Roughness: essentia dissonance (Plomp–Levelt polynomial, FORMULA) and MIRtoolbox Plomp–Levelt parametrisation `exp(−3.51·s·Δf) − exp(−5.75·s·Δf)`, `s = 0.24/(0.0207·f_min + 18.96)` (FORMULA). — [dissonance.cpp](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/23ae7ba/src/algorithms/tonal/dissonance.cpp); [mirroughness.m](https://github.com/olivierlar/mirtoolbox/blob/71d6265/MIRToolbox/mirroughness.m)
- [SRC] (earlier notes) Legato rated calmer and staccato more tense and energetic (articulation study). — [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-perceptual-and-emotional-consequences-of-articulation-in-musi/) [SEC]

### Inferences
- [CALC] **Timing budget, live input:** onset detection 15–30 ms + one display frame (16.7 ms at 60 Hz, 8.3 ms at 120 Hz) + compositor ≈ one frame → about 40–65 ms visual lag; at 60 Hz this sits at or just past the 45 ms detectability edge, so draw a provisional mark on the first frame after an onset (size from the first 15–20 ms) and refine it later (earlier notes), and subtract `outputLatency` look-ahead where the analysed signal reaches the analyser before the speaker.
- [CALC] **Timing budget, files and the app's own playback:** analyse ahead (whole-file pre-analysis or a 50–100 ms delay on the audible path) and schedule each mark to appear 0–20 ms before its audible time — inside the visual-leading tolerance (up to ~125 ms) and away from the strict audio-leading edge.
- [CALC] **Per-layer feature → visual channel** (all per role, normalised to that role's own recent range):
  - *Attack*: rise time from 10 % to 90 % of the envelope peak, or onset-flux slope → edge hardness, spatter, leading-edge pressure. Short rise (< 10 ms) → hard edge; long (> 50 ms) → feathered entry.
  - *Sustain, legato versus staccato*: legato ratio = effective duration (above 0.4 of peak) / inter-onset interval: > 0.8 legato → connected strokes; < 0.4 staccato → separate dabs; also the share of short versus held events from the existing short/held decision.
  - *Roughness/noisiness*: dissonance (tonal parts) and per-band flatness (noise) → angular, broken, splayed marks and spatter spread.
  - *Brightness*: centroid in octaves and high-band contrast → lightness and saturation inside the palette (the timbre–colour link above).
  - *Intensity*: role loudness relative to the song's 95th percentile → size, pressure, paint load; song-level loudness range and energy → global manner only (slow).
