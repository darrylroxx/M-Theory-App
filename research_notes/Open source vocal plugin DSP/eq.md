## 1. For each open-source EQ: licence, author, repo, what the filters are, and how it is regarded

### Takeaway
The directly reusable sources (GPL-3.0-or-later compatible) are: Airwindows and lkjb Luftikus and `ivantsov-filters` (all MIT), x42 fil4 and zam-plugins (GPL-2.0-or-later), LSP dsp-units (LGPL-3.0-or-later), Calf `biquad.h` (LGPL-2.0-or-later), sst-filters and dusk-audio (GPL-3.0-or-later), chowdsp_utils filter modules / BYOD / EQP-WDF-1A / eq1979 (GPL-3.0); ZL Equalizer is AGPL-3.0 and must only be studied. Almost every open EQ still uses plain RBJ/bilinear biquads; true decramping exists only in chowdsp_utils (Vicanek), ZamEQ2 (Orfanidis), ZL Equalizer and `ivantsov-filters` (Ivantsov 2025), and Luftikus (pre-fitted per-sample-rate tables).

**What the filters are, per plugin**
- **Luftikus (Maag EQ4-style)** [SRC]: six *parallel* filters fed from the same input and summed: `out = Σ_n (filt_n(x)·pg_n + x)·g_n`, then `· globalGain` (`0.29`, or `0.398/dcGain` with "keep gain"). Bands: `kBand10, kBand40, kBand160, kBand640` (band-pass biquads), `kShelf2k5`, and one selectable high shelf `kA2k5/kA5k/kA10k/kA20k/kA40k`. No coefficient formulas exist in the code — `coeffcreator.h` is a table of precomputed `double` coefficients for 44.1/48/88.2/96/176.4/192 kHz; the sample rate is snapped to the nearest table (no other rates supported). Direct-form-I biquad with `double` state. No oversampling; "analog" mode only adds ±0.5e-5 white noise to the input and randomises the gain by up to ±0.04 dB. — [coeffcreator.h](https://github.com/lkjbdsp/lkjb-plugins/blob/master/Luftikus/Source/dsp/coeffcreator.h), [eqdsp.cpp](https://github.com/lkjbdsp/lkjb-plugins/blob/master/Luftikus/Source/dsp/eqdsp.cpp), [eqdsp.h](https://github.com/lkjbdsp/lkjb-plugins/blob/master/Luftikus/Source/dsp/eqdsp.h)
- **Luftikus gain law** [SRC]: for the five lower bands, with `x = gain_dB/20 + 0.5` (knob −10…+10): `x>0.5: g = 0.5·56.2/(5.56 + 4011.4·exp(−7.4746·x) − 0.54573), pg=1`; `0.25≤x≤0.5: g = 0.5·56.2/(5.56 + 500 − 810·(x−0.25)·2), pg=1`; `x<0.25: g = 0.5·56.2/(5.56+500), pg = 4x`. High shelf with `x = gain/10` (knob 0…10): `g = 0.5·56.2/(5.56 + (x≤0.5 ? 500 − 823.6·x : 3258.2·exp(−7.4126·x) − 1.8466))`. (The constants look like a resistor-network/pot-taper fit; the code has no comment explaining them.) — [eqdsp.cpp](https://github.com/lkjbdsp/lkjb-plugins/blob/master/Luftikus/Source/dsp/eqdsp.cpp)
- **ZL Equalizer** [SRC]: filter structures enumerated as `kMinimum, kSVF, kParallel, kMatched, kMixed, kZero` (UI names "Matched Phase", "Mixed Phase", "Zero Phase"). The IIR coefficient generator is now `IvantsovCoeff` / `IvantsovSVFCoeff`; the credits cite "Yuriy Ivantsov. On the ideal bilinear and biquadratic digital filter. (2025)", Vicanek's 2016 and 2019 papers, Redmon "Cascading filters" and Wishnick "Time-Varying Filters for Musical Applications" (DAFx 2014). Up to tag 0.5.0 it used a `MartinCoeff` class (Vicanek matched designs). The changelog says State Variable filters "have identical magnitude & phase response compared to Minimum Phase, but different modulation/dynamic behavior", and that Matched/Mixed/Zero Phase "introduce a constant latency". — [zlp_definitions.hpp](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/main/source/zlp/zlp_definitions.hpp), [credit_panel.hpp](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/main/source/panel/ui_setting_panel/credit_panel.hpp), [ivantsov_coeff.cpp](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/main/source/dsp/filter/iir_filter/coeff/ivantsov_coeff.cpp), [martin_coeff.cpp @0.5.0](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/0.5.0/source/dsp/filter/iir_filter/coeff/martin_coeff.cpp), [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)
- **x42 fil4 / Fons Adriaensen `Fil4Paramsect`** [SRC]: the four parametric bands are NOT direct-form biquads; each is a 2nd-order all-pass lattice (Regalia–Mitra style) with `s1 = −cos(2π·f)`, bandwidth term `b·7·f/√g`, `s2 = (1−b)/(1+b)`, mix `a = 0.5·(g−1)`; per sample: `y = x − s2·z2; out = x − a·(z2 + s2·y − x); y −= s1·z1; z2 = z1 + s1·y; z1 = y + 1e-10`. `f` is normalised f/fs, `g` linear gain. All three coefficients are linearly interpolated per sample across the block (`d1=(s1_new−s1)/k` …), and each parameter may change by at most ×2 / ÷2 per call. The shelves (`iir.h`) are RBJ cookbook shelves (Q clamped 0.25–2, freq clamped 0.0004·fs…0.47·fs) run in transposed direct form II, with the *parameters* one-pole smoothed (`lpf = 440/rate`) and coefficients recomputed. The high-pass (`hip.h`) is a custom two-integrator resonant HP with `alpha = exp(−2π·f/fs)`, frequency limited to fs/12. No decramping, no oversampling, no saturation. — [filters.h](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/filters.h), [iir.h](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/iir.h), [hip.h](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/hip.h)
- **ZamEQ2** [SRC]: two peaking bands designed with Orfanidis' prescribed-Nyquist-gain formula (function `peq(G0,G,GB,w0,Dw,…)`, called with `G0 = 1`, `GB = sqrt(G)`, `Dw = fc/Q` in the code's units) plus RBJ low/high shelves using the slope form `alpha = sin(w0)/2·sqrt((A+1/A)(1/q−1)+2)` with q = 0.707. — [ZamEQ2Plugin.cpp](https://github.com/zamaudio/zam-plugins/blob/master/plugins/ZamEQ2/ZamEQ2Plugin.cpp)
- **LSP (Parametric EQ DSP)** [SRC]: every analog prototype family (`RLC`, `BWC` Butterworth-Chebyshev, `LRX` Linkwitz-Riley, `APO`) exists in a `FLT_BT_*` (bilinear transform) and a `FLT_MT_*` (matched Z-transform) variant; `Filter::update` calls `bilinear_transform()` or `matched_transform()`, and the source comment notes "After the Matched Z-transform the Frequency Chart of the filter has to be normalized!". The RLC bell prototype is `(s² + kt·s + 1)/(s² + kb·s + 1)` with `fg = gain^(1/slope)`, `angle = atan(fg)`, `k = 2(1/fg + fg)/(1 + 2Q/slope)`, `kt = k·sin(angle)`, `kb = k·cos(angle)`. `FLT_DR_APO_*` are direct RBJ ("Equalizer APO") designs. — [Filter.cpp](https://github.com/lsp-plugins/lsp-dsp-units/blob/master/src/main/filters/Filter.cpp), [common.h](https://github.com/lsp-plugins/lsp-dsp-units/blob/master/include/lsp-plug.in/dsp-units/filters/common.h)
- **Calf** [SRC]: `biquad.h` is plain RBJ (`set_peakeq_rbj`, `set_lowshelf_rbj`, `set_highshelf_rbj`, `set_hp_rbj`), coefficients computed in double; no decramping. — [biquad.h](https://github.com/calf-studio-gear/calf/blob/master/src/calf/biquad.h)
- **chowdsp_utils** [SRC]: `CoefficientCalculationMode::{Standard, Decramped}`; "Decramped" = Vicanek matched design for LPF/HPF/BPF/notch/peaking, plus the author's own Vicanek-style derivation for low/high shelves ("The Vicanek paper doesn't derive the shelving filters, so here's what we do…"). Two robustness guards: Q is clamped to [0.1, 30) ("Vicanek method has numerical problems for Q-values outside of this range") and **any band with fc < 1000 Hz silently falls back to the bilinear design**. Also has a Simper-style `StateVariableFilter` (bell, shelves, multimode) and a FIR `LinearPhaseEQ`. — [chowdsp_VicanekHelpers.h](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/Utils/chowdsp_VicanekHelpers.h), [chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/Utils/chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h), [chowdsp_StateVariableFilter.cpp](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/LowerOrderFilters/chowdsp_StateVariableFilter.cpp)
- **dusk-audio Multi-Q / 4K EQ** [SRC]: `AnalogMatchedBiquad.h` is *not* a Nyquist-matched design despite the name: the peaking filter is `b = [1+kbw·A, −2cos w0, 1−kbw·A]`, `a = [1+kbw/A, −2cos w0, 1−kbw/A]` with `kbw = tan(π·(fc/Q)/fs)`, `A = 10^(dB/40)`; its own comment lists "H(Nyquist) = 0 dB". Shelves are RBJ slope-form shelves with `cosW,sinW` derived from `k = tan(π fc/fs)`. So it fixes bandwidth warping only; the gain is still forced to 0 dB at Nyquist. — [AnalogMatchedBiquad.h](https://github.com/dusk-audio/dusk-audio-plugins/blob/main/plugins/multi-q/AnalogMatchedBiquad.h)
- **EQP-WDF-1A (Pultec)** [SRC]: wave-digital-filter model of the passive EQP-1A network built with `chowdsp_wdf` (series/parallel adaptors, ideal voltage source root), fixed make-up gain `15.4882`, oversampling selectable x1/x2/x4/x8/x16 (default index 1 = x2) using JUCE half-band equiripple FIR stages, latency reported to the host via `setLatencySamples(oversampler.getLatencyInSamples())`. No tube/transformer saturation model. — [EQP1A.h](https://github.com/ABSounds/EQP-WDF-1A/blob/main/Source/EQP1A.h), [EQP1A.cpp](https://github.com/ABSounds/EQP-WDF-1A/blob/main/Source/EQP1A.cpp), [PluginProcessor.cpp](https://github.com/ABSounds/EQP-WDF-1A/blob/main/Source/PluginProcessor.cpp)
- **EQ1979 (Neve 1073-style)** [SRC]: "Designed by cascading ~30 peq/butterworth filters to reproduce frequency response, bumps and drops of the original console strip"; all sections are RBJ biquads (peak/low-shelf/high-shelf) and Butterworth 2nd-order HP/LP, plus gain-compensated arctan saturation, bias asymmetry `0.0021` and noise floor `3.21e-6`. Parameters are low-passed at 10 Hz and coefficients recomputed every sample. — [bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx](https://github.com/D4p0up/eq1979/blob/main/bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx)
- **Airwindows** (all [SRC], all bilinear/no decramping, `double` maths, 32-bit-float dither on output):
  - *Baxandall2*: not a tone-stack model — a complementary split. `trebleGain = 10^((A·48−24)/20)`, `trebleFreq = 4410·trebleGain/fs` (max 0.45), Q 0.4; `bassGain = 10^((B·48−24)/20)`, `bassFreq = 8820·10^(−(B·48−24)/20)/fs` (max 0.45), Q 0.2. Both are bilinear low-pass biquads (`K = tan(π·f)`, `norm = 1/(1+K/Q+K²)`, `b0 = K²·norm, b1 = 2b0, b2 = b0, a1 = 2(K²−1)·norm, a2 = (1−K/Q+K²)·norm`); `treble = (x − LP_t(x))·trebleGain`, `bass = LP_b(x)·bassGain`, `out = bass + treble`. So the corner frequencies move with gain (more treble boost → higher treble corner; more bass boost → lower bass corner). — [Baxandall2Proc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/Baxandall2/Baxandall2Proc.cpp)
  - *Air3*: not a biquad shelf; a polynomial extrapolation ("pvA/pvS/acc" differences) that predicts the next sample, splits the signal into "air" and "ground" parts and re-mixes them: `out = (dry − gnd)·airGain + gnd·gndGain`, `airGain = 2A` (raised to the power `3+sqrt(fs/44100)` above 1). — [Air3Proc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/Air3/Air3Proc.cpp)
  - *Weight*: bass-boost built from 8 cascaded Holt (double-exponential "trend/forecast") smoothers: `targetFreq = ((A+0.53)·0.2)/sqrt(fs/44100)`, `alpha = targetFreq⁴`, `beta = alpha·(0.05·B+0.2)²`, `alpha += (1−beta)·targetFreq³`; output = dry + filtered·B. — [WeightProc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/Weight/WeightProc.cpp)
  - *Highpass2*: up to four cascaded one-pole high-passes (`iir = iir·(1−o·amt) + x·o·amt; x −= iir`) where `o` is made signal-dependent by the "tight" control (`o = (1−tight) + |x|·tight`), i.e. a level-dependent corner frequency. — [Highpass2Proc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/Highpass2/Highpass2Proc.cpp)
  - *Parametric*: three bands (high `((A³·14500)+1500)` Hz, hi-mid `((D³·6400)+600)` Hz, low-mid `((G³·2200)+20)` Hz) each made of three bilinear band-pass biquads with Q multiplied by `1.93185165, 0.70710678, 0.51763809` (6th-order Butterworth pole Qs). — [ParametricProc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/Parametric/ParametricProc.cpp)
  - *MackEQ*: four bilinear low-pass biquads at `19160/fs` with Q `0.431684981684982, 1.1582298, 0.657027382751269, 1.076210852946577`, plus one-pole IIR band splits (`iirBassMid = 0.159/overallscale`, `iirMidHigh = 0.236/overallscale`). — [MackEQProc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/MackEQ/MackEQProc.cpp)
  - *Capacitor2*: one-pole low/high-pass pairs whose control values "chase" the knob (`lowpassSpeed = 300/(|Δ|+1)`), with a nonlinearity control. *Holt*: Holt-smoothing resonant low-pass. *Pafnuty*: Chebyshev-polynomial harmonic generator (2nd–13th), not an EQ. — [Capacitor2Proc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/Capacitor2/Capacitor2Proc.cpp), [HoltProc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/Holt/HoltProc.cpp), [PafnutyProc.cpp](https://github.com/airwindows/airwindows/blob/master/plugins/LinuxVST/src/Pafnuty/PafnutyProc.cpp)
- **BYOD Baxandall** [SRC]: WDF R-type-adaptor model with pots `Pt = Pb = 100 kΩ`, `Resd 10 kΩ + Cd 6.4 nF`, `Rese 1 kΩ + Ce 64 nF`, `Rl 1 MΩ`, `Resc 10 kΩ`, `Resb 1 kΩ`, `Cc 220 nF`, `Cb 22 nF`, `Resa 10 kΩ`, `Ca 1 µF`. — [BaxandallWDF.h](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/BYOD/blob/main/src/processors/tone/baxandall/BaxandallWDF.h)

### Inferences
- For a GPL-3.0-or-later Rust DAW the cleanest reference set is: `ivantsov-filters` (MIT) and Luftikus (MIT) for decramped/air designs, the Simper SVF via the MIT/Apache `simper-filter` crate, ZamEQ2 (GPL-2.0-or-later) for Orfanidis, and chowdsp_utils (GPLv3) for Vicanek. All of these are re-implementations of published formulas, so re-deriving them in Rust and citing the paper avoids any licence entanglement.
- "Analog-matched" is used loosely in open code. Only designs that leave the Nyquist gain free (Vicanek, Orfanidis 1997, Ivantsov, fitted tables) actually remove cramping; dusk-audio's header and the RBJ/bilinear family do not.
- No widely used open-source EQ models Neve/API/SSL *circuits*; the open ones (EQ1979, dusk-audio) are curve-fits or heuristics. The Pultec is the exception (EQP-WDF-1A is a real circuit model).

## 2. Complete coefficient formulas for analog-matched second-order sections; accuracy at 48 kHz versus RBJ; best practical choice for zero-latency real time

### Takeaway
At 48 kHz a +6 dB bell at 8 kHz (Q 1) designed with RBJ is up to 1.1 dB wrong in the 12–20 kHz skirt and a +6 dB RBJ shelf at 12 kHz is up to 0.9 dB wrong, whereas Vicanek, Orfanidis and Ivantsov designs stay within about 0.2–0.35 dB (0.08 dB for the Vicanek-style shelf). The best practical choice is a matched *minimum-phase biquad* (Vicanek for bells/HP/LP, a Vicanek-style or Ivantsov design for shelves) computed in f64 at control rate — zero latency, one biquad of CPU — with plain pre-warped bilinear/SVF designs below roughly 1 kHz where cramping is negligible and the matched formulas lose precision.

### Cited Findings

**Notation**: `w0 = 2π·fc/fs`, `A = 10^(dB/40)`, `G = 10^(dB/20) = A²`, biquad `H(z) = (b0 + b1 z⁻¹ + b2 z⁻²)/(1 + a1 z⁻¹ + a2 z⁻²)`.

**(a) RBJ Audio-EQ-Cookbook (bilinear) — baseline** [SRC via implementations; the W3C cookbook page itself was blocked]
- Peaking, `alpha = sin(w0)/(2Q)`: `b0 = 1+alpha·A, b1 = −2cos w0, b2 = 1−alpha·A, a0 = 1+alpha/A, a1 = −2cos w0, a2 = 1−alpha/A` (divide by a0). — [Calf biquad.h `set_peakeq_rbj`](https://github.com/calf-studio-gear/calf/blob/master/src/calf/biquad.h), [EQ1979 `_eq_compute_peak_coeffs`](https://github.com/D4p0up/eq1979/blob/main/bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx)
- Low shelf, `alpha = sin(w0)/(2Q)`, `c = cos w0`, `As = sqrt(A)`: `b0 = A((A+1) − (A−1)c + 2As·alpha)`, `b1 = 2A((A−1) − (A+1)c)`, `b2 = A((A+1) − (A−1)c − 2As·alpha)`, `a0 = (A+1) + (A−1)c + 2As·alpha`, `a1 = −2((A−1) + (A+1)c)`, `a2 = (A+1) + (A−1)c − 2As·alpha`. — [fil4 iir.h `iir_calc_lowshelf`](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/iir.h)
- High shelf: `b0 = A((A+1) + (A−1)c + 2As·alpha)`, `b1 = −2A((A−1) + (A+1)c)`, `b2 = A((A+1) + (A−1)c − 2As·alpha)`, `a0 = (A+1) − (A−1)c + 2As·alpha`, `a1 = 2((A−1) − (A+1)c)`, `a2 = (A+1) − (A−1)c − 2As·alpha`. — [fil4 iir.h `iir_calc_highshelf`](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/iir.h)
- Shelf-slope variant: `alpha = sin(w0)/2 · sqrt((A + 1/A)(1/S − 1) + 2)`. — [ZamEQ2Plugin.cpp `lowshelfeq`](https://github.com/zamaudio/zam-plugins/blob/master/plugins/ZamEQ2/ZamEQ2Plugin.cpp)
- High-pass: `b0 = (1+cos w0)/2, b1 = −(1+cos w0), b2 = b0, a0 = 1+alpha, a1 = −2cos w0, a2 = 1−alpha`. — [Calf biquad.h `set_hp_rbj`](https://github.com/calf-studio-gear/calf/blob/master/src/calf/biquad.h)
- Equivalent `K = tan(π fc/fs)` forms: HP `norm = 1/(K² + K/Q + 1)`, `b = [norm, −2norm, norm]`, `a1 = 2(K²−1)norm`, `a2 = (K² − K/Q + 1)norm`; LP `b = [K²norm, 2K²norm, K²norm]`, same `a`. — [dusk AnalogMatchedBiquad.h](https://github.com/dusk-audio/dusk-audio-plugins/blob/main/plugins/multi-q/AnalogMatchedBiquad.h)
- Analog prototypes that these designs approximate: peaking `H(s) = (s² + s·A/Q + 1)/(s² + s/(A·Q) + 1)`; low shelf `A·(s² + (√A/Q)s + A)/(A s² + (√A/Q)s + 1)`; high shelf `A·(A s² + (√A/Q)s + 1)/(s² + (√A/Q)s + A)`. — [chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h doc comments](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/Utils/chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h)

**(b) Vicanek "Matched Second Order Digital Filters" (2016) — as implemented in chowdsp_utils** [SRC for the code; paper not opened]
- Poles (impulse-invariant, i.e. exactly the analog pole positions mapped by `z = e^{sT}`): `q = 1/(2Q)`; `a1 = −2·e^{−q·w0}·cos(sqrt(1−q²)·w0)` if `q ≤ 1`, else `−2·e^{−q·w0}·cosh(sqrt(q²−1)·w0)`; `a2 = e^{−2q·w0}`.
- Helper quantities: `φ1 = sin²(w0/2)`, `φ0 = 1 − φ1`, `φ2 = 4·φ0·φ1`; `A0 = (1+a1+a2)²`, `A1 = (1−a1+a2)²`, `A2 = −4·a2`.
- Generic numerator from `B0, B1, B2`: `W = (√B0 + √B1)/2`; `b0 = (W + sqrt(W² + B2))/2`; `b1 = (√B0 − √B1)/2`; `b2 = −B2/(4·b0)`.
- **Peaking** (prototype `(s² + G·s/Q + 1)/(s² + s/Q + 1)`, G ≥ 1 linear): `R1 = (A0φ0 + A1φ1 + A2φ2)·G²`; `R2 = (−A0 + A1 + 4(φ0−φ1)A2)·G²`; `B0 = A0`; `B2 = (R1 − R2·φ1 − B0)/(4φ1²)`; `B1 = R2 + B0 + 4(φ1−φ0)·B2`; then the generic numerator. For cuts chowdsp designs the boost `1/G` and swaps numerator and denominator ("The Vicanek paper asserts that the zeros will be inside the unit circle, so we can safely invert").
- **High-pass**: `b0 = sqrt(A0φ0 + A1φ1 + A2φ2)·Q/(4φ1)`, `b1 = −2b0`, `b2 = b0`.
- **Low-pass**: `R1 = (A0φ0 + A1φ1 + A2φ2)·Q²`; `B0 = A0`; `B1 = (R1 − B0φ0)/φ1`; `b0 = (√B0 + √B1)/2`; `b1 = √B0 − b0`; `b2 = 0`.
- **Band-pass**: `R1 = A0φ0 + A1φ1 + A2φ2`; `R2 = −A0 + A1 + 4(φ0−φ1)A2`; `B2 = (R1 − R2φ1)/(4φ1²)`; `B1 = R2 + 4(φ1−φ0)B2`; `b1 = −√B1/2`; `b0 = (sqrt(B2 + B1/4) − b1)/2`; `b2 = −b0 − b1`.
- **Notch**: `b = scale·[1, −2cos w0, 1]`, `scale = sqrt(A0)/(2 − 2cos w0)`.
- Guards in that code: `Q` clamped to `[0.1, 30)`; `fc < 1000 Hz` → standard bilinear design instead.
- Source for all of (b): [chowdsp_VicanekHelpers.h](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/Utils/chowdsp_VicanekHelpers.h), [chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/Utils/chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h); paper (not read): [BiquadFits.pdf](https://www.vicanek.de/articles/BiquadFits.pdf)
- To get the RBJ-symmetric convention (same curve for boost and cut, bandwidth `fc/Q` at the half-gain points) call the Vicanek peaking design with pole Q `= Q·A` and gain `G = A²`, and invert for cuts. [NUM — this is what I used in the comparison below] — formula source: [chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/Utils/chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h)

**(c) Vicanek-style matched 2nd-order shelves (chowdsp's own derivation)** [SRC]
- High shelf, analog prototype `A(As² + (√A/Q)s + 1)/(s² + (√A/Q)s + A)`. Always design the *cut* and invert for boosts: `g = min(G, 1/G)`; `α = √g`; `β = √α/(2Q)`; poles `e = exp(−β·w0)`, `a1 = −2e·cos(sqrt(α−β²)·w0)` (or `cosh(sqrt(β²−α)·w0)` when `β² > α`), `a2 = e²`; Nyquist-gain target evaluated on the analog curve slightly below Nyquist: `f0 = 2π/w0`, `wN² = (0.49·f0)²`, `C = (β·f0)²`, `Gn² = g·((1 − α·wN²)² + C)/((α − wN²)² + C)`; then `B0 = A0` (DC gain 1), `B1 = A1·Gn²` (Nyquist gain), `R1 = (A0φ0 + A1φ1 + A2φ2)·g` (gain at fc), `B2 = (R1 − B0φ0 − B1φ1)/φ2`; generic numerator; if `G > 1` swap numerator/denominator and renormalise.
- Low shelf, prototype `A(s² + (√A/Q)s + A)/(As² + (√A/Q)s + 1)`: `g = max(G, 1/G)`, `α = √g`, `β = √α/(2Q)`; poles `e = exp(−β·w0/α)`, `a1 = −2e·cos(sqrt(α−β²)·w0/α)` (or cosh), `a2 = e²`; `wN² = (0.5·f0)²`, `C = (β·f0)²`, `G2 = g·((α − wN²)² + C)/((1 − α·wN²)² + C)`; `B0 = A0·g²`, `B1 = A1·G2`, `R1 = (A0φ0 + A1φ1 + A2φ2)·g`, `B2 = (R1 − B0φ0 − B1φ1)/φ2`; invert when `G < 1`.
- Source: [chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h `calcHighShelf` / `calcLowShelf`](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/Utils/chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h). Vicanek published his own "Matched Two-Pole Digital Shelving Filters" (23 March 2024) which I could not open: [2poleShelvingFits.pdf](https://www.vicanek.de/articles/2poleShelvingFits.pdf) [SEC for its existence/date].

**(d) Orfanidis "Digital Parametric Equalizer Design with Prescribed Nyquist-Frequency Gain" (JAES 1997) — as implemented in ZamEQ2** [SRC for the code; paper not opened]
- Inputs: `G0` reference gain (1), `G` peak gain (linear), `GB` gain at the bandwidth edges (ZamEQ2 uses `GB = sqrt(G)`), `w0` centre (rad/sample), `Dw` bandwidth (rad/sample).
- `F = |G² − GB²|`; `G00 = |G² − G0²|`; `F00 = |GB² − G0²|`
- `G1 = sqrt( (G0²(w0² − π²)² + G²·F00·π²·Dw²/F) / ((w0² − π²)² + F00·π²·Dw²/F) )`  ← the prescribed Nyquist gain = the analog filter's gain at Nyquist
- `G01 = |G² − G0·G1|`; `G11 = |G² − G1²|`; `F01 = |GB² − G0·G1|`; `F11 = |GB² − G1²|`
- `W2 = sqrt(G11/G00)·tan²(w0/2)`; `DW = (1 + sqrt(F00/F11)·W2)·tan(Dw/2)`
- `C = F11·DW² − 2·W2·(F01 − sqrt(F00·F11))`; `D = 2·W2·(G01 − sqrt(G00·G11))`
- `A = sqrt((C + D)/F)`; `B = sqrt((G²·C + GB²·D)/F)`
- `b0 = (G1 + G0·W2 + B)/(1 + W2 + A)`; `b1 = −2(G1 − G0·W2)/(1 + W2 + A)`; `b2 = (G1 − B + G0·W2)/(1 + W2 + A)`; `a1 = −2(1 − W2)/(1 + W2 + A)`; `a2 = (1 + W2 − A)/(1 + W2 + A)`
- Source: [ZamEQ2Plugin.cpp `peq`](https://github.com/zamaudio/zam-plugins/blob/master/plugins/ZamEQ2/ZamEQ2Plugin.cpp). Note the formula divides by `F` and `G00`, so `G = 1` (0 dB) must be special-cased (ZamEQ2 falls back to `b0 = 1` when the result is not normal).

**(e) Ivantsov "On the ideal bilinear and biquadratic digital filter" (2025) — as implemented by the author (MIT)** [SRC for the code; paper not opened]
- Parameters: `w = fs/(π·fc)` (note: inverse frequency), damping `ζ = 1/(2Q)`, warp constant `σ = 2/π` by default.
- Cut-off corrections (`Correction::Omega` / `Correction::Sigma`), with `ctg = 1/tan(1/w) = cot(π·fc/fs)`: Omega: `w ← sqrt(σ² + ctg²)`; Sigma: `σ ← sqrt(w² − ctg²)` (the code computes this in at least double because "Sigma correction can fail on float for small fc").
- Shelf gain: for an order-N filter `g = G^(1/N)` (so for a biquad `g = √G`, G linear amplitude gain).
- Helper `vk(t, u)`: `k = t·(2u − 1)`; `v = sqrt(t² + σ²·(2k + σ²))`; returns `(v, k + σ²)`.
- Denominator pair: `(v, k) = vk(w²·g, ζ²)` for LowShelf, `vk(w²/g, ζ²)` for HighShelf, `vk(w², ζ²)` otherwise (for BandShelf/bell the damping used is `ζ/g`). Then `c0 = 2/(1 + sqrt(2(k+v)) + v)`, `c1 = sqrt(v)`.
- Numerator pair: `(va, ka) = vk(w²/g, ζ²)` LowShelf; `vk(w²·g, ζ²)` HighShelf; `vk(w², (ζ·g)²)` BandShelf (bell).
- `c2 =` HighPass `w²/c1` | LowPass `σ²/c1` | BandPass `2wζσ/c1` | BandStop `(w² − σ²)/c1` | AllPass `c1` | shelves/bell `va/c1`.
- `c3 =` HighPass `w²/2` | LowPass `(1+σ)²/2` | BandPass `wζ(1+σ)` | BandStop `(1 + w² − σ²)/2` | AllPass `(1 − sqrt(2(k+v)) + v)/2` | shelves/bell `(1 + sqrt(2(ka+va)) + va)/2`.
- Per-sample recursion (its own 2-state structure, not a direct form): `θ = (x − z0 − z1·c1)·c0`; `y = θ·c3 + z1·c2` (HighPass, BandPass) or `y = θ·c3 + z1·c2 + z0` (all others); `z0 += θ`; `z1 = −z1 − θ·c1`; LowShelf output is multiplied by `g²`.
- Closed-form magnitude with `x = cos(2πf/fs)`, `d = 1 − x`, `F(t,u) = (1 + x − u·d)² + (2t − u − 1)²·d·(1 + x)`: `|H| = sqrt(F(c3, c1·c2)/F(1/c0, c1²))` (special numerators for HP/BP).
- First-order section: `v(t) = sqrt(t + σ²)`; `c0 = 2/(1 + v(w²/G))` (HighShelf), `2/(1 + v(w²·G))` (LowShelf), `2/(1 + v(w²))` (others); `c1 = (1 + v(w²·G))/2` (HighShelf), `(1 + v(w²/G))/2` (LowShelf), `(1 − v(w²))/2` (AllPass), `(1 + σ)/2` (LowPass), `w/2` (HighPass). Recursion: `θ = (x − z)·c0; y = θ·c1 (+ z unless HighPass); z += θ`; LowShelf output × G.
- Source for all of (e): [ivantsov-filters filters.h](https://github.com/yIvantsov/ivantsov-filters/blob/main/filters.h); paper links given in its README (not reachable here): [README](https://github.com/yIvantsov/ivantsov-filters/blob/main/README.md)
- My Python port of the 2nd-order section reproduces its own closed-form magnitude to 7e-15 dB when the impulse response of the recursion is FFT'd, so the port used for the numbers below is faithful. [NUM] — [filters.h](https://github.com/yIvantsov/ivantsov-filters/blob/main/filters.h)
- ZL Equalizer (AGPL, study only) uses the same family with a slightly retuned warp constant (`kSigma = 2.00143` in units scaled by π, i.e. 0.63707 versus 2/π = 0.63662), the Omega-type correction `w² = σ² + π²/tan²(π·f/fs)` for `f/fs ≤ 0.49964`, **no correction (`w² = (fs/f)²`) for `f/fs ≥ 0.592`** — i.e. corner frequencies above Nyquist are allowed — and a cubic blend between. — [ivantsov_common.hpp](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/main/source/dsp/filter/iir_filter/coeff/ivantsov_common.hpp)

**(g) State-variable (Simper/Cytomic trapezoidal) form — for smooth modulation** [SRC via two independent implementations; Cytomic PDF blocked]
- Coefficients: `g = tan(π·fc/fs)`, `k = 1/Q`, `a1 = 1/(1 + g·(g + k))`, `a2 = g·a1`, `a3 = g·a2`.
- Tick: `v3 = v0 − ic2eq; v1 = a1·ic1eq + a2·v3; v2 = ic2eq + a2·ic1eq + a3·v3; ic1eq = 2·v1 − ic1eq; ic2eq = 2·v2 − ic2eq; out = m0·v0 + m1·v1 + m2·v2`.
- Bell: `A = 10^(dB/40)`, `k = 1/(Q·A)`, `m0 = 1, m1 = k·(A² − 1), m2 = 0`.
- Low shelf: `g = tan(π·fc/fs)/√A`, `k = 1/Q`, `m0 = 1, m1 = k·(A − 1), m2 = A² − 1`.
- High shelf: `g = tan(π·fc/fs)·√A`, `k = 1/Q`, `m0 = A², m1 = k·(1 − A)·A, m2 = 1 − A²`.
- Source: [simper-filter lib.rs](https://docs.rs/simper-filter/0.1.1/src/simper_filter/lib.rs.html) (states it is "copied from Andrew Simper (Cytomic) https://cytomic.com/files/dsp/SvfLinearTrapOptimised2.pdf"); the same structure with `g/√A`, `g·√A`, `k/A` scaling is in [chowdsp_StateVariableFilter.cpp](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/LowerOrderFilters/chowdsp_StateVariableFilter.cpp) and [chowdsp_StateVariableFilter.h](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/LowerOrderFilters/chowdsp_StateVariableFilter.h) (`Bell: v2 + v0 + k0·A·v1`, `LowShelf: A²·v2 + k0·A·v1 + v0`, `HighShelf: A²·v0 + k0·A·v1 + v2`).
- These SVF designs have exactly the bilinear (cramped) magnitude response — ZL's changelog says the same about its State Variable mode versus its Minimum Phase mode. — [ZL CHANGELOG](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)
- High-pass output `v0 − k·v1 − v2` reproduces the RBJ Butterworth high-pass to −118 dBFS (f32 vs f64 reference) in my test. [NUM] — structure per [simper-filter lib.rs](https://docs.rs/simper-filter/0.1.1/src/simper_filter/lib.rs.html)

**(h) Minimum-phase versus linear-phase, and decramping by oversampling**
- ZL Equalizer's non-minimum-phase modes ("Matched Phase", "Mixed Phase", "Zero Phase") "introduce a constant latency", while its minimum-phase and SVF structures do not. [SRC] — [ZL CHANGELOG](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)

### Inferences
- **Recommended choice**: matched minimum-phase biquads. Use Vicanek for bells, high-pass and low-pass (smallest error in the presence/air region for Q ≥ 0.7, error only grows in the last few hundred Hz below Nyquist), and either the chowdsp Vicanek-style derivation or Ivantsov-Omega for 2nd-order shelves. If one uniform code path is preferred, Ivantsov with Omega correction gives ≤ 0.35 dB for every filter type tested, needs only square roots plus one `tan`, has an MIT reference, and is the only one of the four that also accepts corner frequencies above Nyquist. Orfanidis is excellent for narrow/moderate bells below ~12 kHz but is the weakest for wide bells near Nyquist and covers bells only.
- Why not oversampling: a 2x oversampled bilinear EQ still cramps (just one octave higher) and needs anti-alias filters — latency if they are linear-phase, extra phase shift if minimum-phase — for a linear process that gains nothing else from oversampling. Matched coefficient design gets the same result with zero latency and no extra CPU per sample.
- Why minimum phase for a live vocal: an analog EQ is minimum-phase, so a matched minimum-phase biquad reproduces both magnitude *and* (very nearly) phase of the hardware being emulated; linear-phase needs a symmetric FIR, which costs latency of half the kernel length (unusable for monitoring through the chain) and adds pre-ringing on boosts. (Reasoned from standard filter theory plus the ZL/EQP-WDF-1A latency facts above; I did not fetch a dedicated article on this.)
- Interpretation of Ivantsov's design (my reading of the magnitude formula in `filters.h`): the digital magnitude equals the analog magnitude evaluated at a warped frequency `Ω² = w²·T²/(1 + σ²·T²)` with `T = tan(πf/fs)`; near DC this is the identity, and with `σ = 2/π` the warp saturates so that Nyquist maps exactly onto the analog Nyquist frequency instead of infinity. That is why the uncorrected version is exact at Nyquist (errors of −0.01 dB at 23.9 kHz in the table runs) but shifts mid-band features by a few percent, and why the Omega correction trades the exact Nyquist gain for an exact corner frequency.
- **Running a matched biquad inside the SVF structure** [DERIVED, verified to 1e-16 on impulse responses for Vicanek bells, the chowdsp-style shelf, Orfanidis and a Vicanek high-pass]: any stable biquad `(b0,b1,b2,a1,a2)` maps to the Simper SVF with `g = sqrt((1+a1+a2)/(1−a1+a2))`, `k = 2(1−a2)/((1−a1+a2)·g)`, `m0 = (b0−b1+b2)/(1−a1+a2)` (= Nyquist gain), `m2 = (b0+b1+b2)/(1+a1+a2) − m0` (= DC gain − m0), `m1 = ((b0−b2)·(1+k·g+g²) − 2·k·g·m0)/(2g)`. This gives decramped responses with the SVF's better low-frequency noise and modulation behaviour, and is presumably what ZL's `IvantsovSVFCoeff` does (not confirmed — I did not read that file).

### Gaps
- The Vicanek, Orfanidis, Ivantsov and Cytomic papers themselves were not readable; equation numbers and the authors' own error plots are therefore not quoted. The formulas above are what the cited code implements.
- I did not find/read an open implementation of Orfanidis' *shelving* designs or his 2005 high-order paper; "n/a" in the table means not tested, not impossible.
- Phase-response error of each matched design versus analog was not measured (magnitude only).
- Zavalishin's "The Art of VA Filter Design" was not opened (native-instruments.com blocked); the TPT/SVF statements rest on the Simper-derived code.

## 3. How to implement a Maag-style 20 kHz / 40 kHz air shelf at 48 kHz (corner at or above Nyquist) — what the open implementations do

### Takeaway
Luftikus solves it with pre-computed, per-sample-rate tables: each air setting is a *first-order* high-pass (pole placed by the matched-Z rule `exp(−2π·fc/fs)`, which is valid for any fc, plus a zero at DC and a small real correction zero) summed in parallel with the dry signal; the labelled "20 k/40 k" bands actually have pole frequencies of 16.93 kHz and 36.17 kHz. The same curve can be generated at any sample rate from a matched one-pole shelf formula (Vicanek one-pole or Ivantsov uncorrected), which stay within ~0.15–0.5 dB of the analog shelf even with the corner at 40 kHz and fs = 44.1/48 kHz.

### Inferences
- The audible effect of a "40 kHz" air band is the *lower skirt* of a first-order shelf: a smooth 6 dB/octave tilt that is still rising at 20 kHz. That is why it sounds "airy without harshness" — it puts most of its boost above the 5–9 kHz sibilance band. Any implementation must therefore be first-order (not a 12 dB/oct RBJ shelf) and must not be forced to 0 dB or to a plateau at Nyquist.
- Simplest faithful Rust implementation at arbitrary sample rate: `air(x) = x + k·HP1(x)` or directly a matched one-pole shelf with `G = 1 + k`; compute the pole as `p = exp(−2π·fc/fs)` (Luftikus' rule, valid above Nyquist) or use the Vicanek one-pole formula; use Luftikus' pole frequencies (1989, 3979, 7958, 16931, 36172 Hz) if the goal is to match that plugin, or the nominal 2.5/5/10/20/40 kHz if the goal is the labelled behaviour.
- Luftikus' second numerator zero (0.03 at 20 k, 0.076 at 40 k, 48 kHz) is evidently a fitted magnitude correction on top of the matched-Z pole; a matched one-pole formula produces the equivalent correction analytically. (Inference from the pole/zero analysis; lkjb's design script is not in the repo.)
- To reproduce the Maag/Luftikus "everything gets louder" interaction, sum the bands in parallel with the dry path as in `eqdsp.cpp`; to avoid it (probably preferable in a vocal chain with a compressor after it), use a normal series shelf with unity DC gain.

### Gaps
- No Maag schematic or published transfer function was found/read; the hardware figures are from a review and a manual summary, and the EQ4 (500-series) versus EQ4M numbers differ between sources (+20 dB vs +15/+17 dB max air gain).
- lkjb's method for generating the tables (and the origin of the gain-law constants) is not documented in the repository.
- The exact fm constant and equation numbers of Vicanek's one-pole paper are from memory; verify against the PDF before quoting it as the paper's formula (the numeric behaviour was verified).

## 4. Modelling Pultec-style simultaneous boost/cut and Neve-style inductor bells simply but convincingly

### Takeaway
The Pultec EQP-1A passive network reduces exactly to simple analog sections: the low boost + attenuate pair is one biquad with real poles/zeros whose coefficients follow directly from the two pot resistances, the high boost is an exact peaking biquad whose pole and zero Q both depend on the bandwidth and boost pots (so peak gain and Q interact), and the high attenuate is a first-order shelf/low-pass. For the Neve 1073 no open circuit model was found; the only open source is a GPL curve-fit (EQ1979) built from RBJ sections with gain-dependent Q and centre frequency.

- **HF shelf ("12 kHz", ±16 dB)**: RBJ high shelf at **2000 Hz with Q 0.35**, gain = knob, plus a compensating peak at 820 Hz, Q 0.45, gain `= −3.5/18 × knob` dB.
- **Mid bell**: main peak `eq(f_main, Q, gain)` with `f_main` = 358, 750, 1590, 3200 Hz for the first four positions and **gain-dependent centre** `5800 − 600·|g|/18` Hz ("4.8 k") and `9400 − 2130·|g|/18` Hz ("7.2 k"); **gain-dependent Q** `0.22 + |g|/60`, `/55`, `/60`, `/72`, `/35`, `/32` for the six positions (g in dB); plus a fixed +1 dB, Q 0.25 "counter bump" at 380, 700, 1600, 3200, 6800, 12200 Hz.
- **LF shelf**: modelled as a very wide *bell*: `eq(30/32/50/80 Hz, Q 0.22/0.12/0.12/0.12, gain)` for the 35/60/110/220 positions, plus a bump `+(0.5 + n/8)` dB, Q 0.25 at 35/80/130/240 Hz and a "drop" peak at `(240/420/540/1000 + 5·|gain|)` Hz, Q 0.5, gain `−0.5 − n/11 − gain/(2.6 + 2/n)` dB (n = switch position 1–4).
- **High-pass**: two cascaded 2nd-order Butterworth high-passes at 34 / 64 / 132 / 270 Hz for the 50 / 80 / 160 / 300 positions, plus a resonance peak (+1.1 dB Q 1.6 @ 46 Hz; +2.4 dB Q 1.2 @ 76 Hz; +2.2 dB Q 1.9 @ 160 Hz; +2.4 dB Q 1.6 @ 330 Hz) and a small dip (−0.3 dB Q 0.7 @ 78 Hz; −1.6 dB Q 0.7 @ 110 Hz; −0.5 dB Q 1 @ 302 Hz; −0.4 dB Q 0.7 @ 665 Hz).
- Saturation: `sat_S = 10·exp((2·(amount/10) − 20)/e)`, `y = sat_trim·atan(x·sat_S)/atan(sat_S)`, `sat_trim = 1 − 0.5·atan(sat_S)`, with a bias term `0.0021` and noise `3.21e-6`.
- — [bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx](https://github.com/D4p0up/eq1979/blob/main/bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx)

**SSL E/G and API proportional-Q — open code found** [SRC for the code; heuristic]
- dusk-audio "British" mode: Brown (E-series) = constant user Q, shelves with `Q·0.65`; Black (G-series) = shelf `Q·1.4` and gain-dependent bell Q: `dynamicQ = baseQ·(1 + (|gain|/20)·scale)` with `scale = 2.0` for boosts and `1.5` for cuts, clamped to 0.5–8, then additionally `Q·(1 + (|gain|/15)·1.2)` for boosts / `·0.6` for cuts inside the peak routine; high-pass = 1st-order + 2nd-order (Q 1.0) = 18 dB/oct; low-pass 12 dB/oct with Q 0.707 (Brown) / 0.8 (Black). — [BritishEQProcessor.h](https://github.com/dusk-audio/dusk-audio-plugins/blob/main/plugins/multi-q/BritishEQProcessor.h)
- API 550A (search summary of API's own page): "reciprocal equalization at 21 points in 5 steps of boost to a maximum of 12dB", steps of 2, 4, 6, 9 and 12 dB, LF points 30/40/50/100/200/300/400 Hz, HF points 2.5/5/7/10 kHz …(list truncated in the snippet); proportional Q = the bandwidth narrows as boost/cut increases. [SEC; other sources quoted in the same summary disagree on the number of frequency points] — [API 550A product page](https://apiaudio.com/product/550a-discrete-3-band-eq/)
- SSL E-series variants (retailer listings/forum posts via search): "Black" EQ ±18 dB versus "Brown" ±15 dB; a forum post says the Brown-knob EQ has 12 dB/oct high- and low-pass filters. [SEC, weak sources] — [zZounds SSL E-EQ listing](https://www.zzounds.com/item--SOLEEQMK2500), [Gearspace thread](https://gearspace.com/threads/different-ssl-eqs.23018/)

### Inferences
- **Simple, convincing Pultec low end**: implement the derived LF biquad directly — map the two knobs to `R_b` (0–8.2 kΩ) and `R_c` (0–110 kΩ), compute the six analog coefficients, and digitise with the bilinear transform (all poles/zeros are real and below ~1.5 kHz, so warping is negligible at 44.1 kHz and up). One biquad then reproduces boost, attenuation and the "trick" exactly as the WDF model does, at zero latency and without oversampling. Pot taper is a free design choice (the WDF plugin uses a skew of 0.3; SOS's "+3 dB shelf / −2 dB dip at 2.5/2.5" gives a calibration point for a hardware-like taper).
- **Pultec high boost**: use a matched (Vicanek) peaking biquad with *separate* pole and zero Q from the closed form: `Qp = 3464.1/(Rq + Rs∥Rh)`, `Qz = 3464.1/(Rq + Rh)`. This automatically yields the hardware's coupling (sharp = +20 dB narrow, broad = +10 dB wide). The Vicanek peaking prototype `(s² + G·s/Q + 1)/(s² + s/Q + 1)` takes exactly these as `Q = Qp`, `G = Qp/Qz`.
- **Neve-style bell** without a circuit model: a matched bell whose Q rises with |gain| (EQ1979's fit: Q ≈ 0.22 at 0 dB to ≈ 0.5 at ±18 dB for the lower positions, steeper for the top two) and whose two highest centres slide down with gain. In the RBJ/Vicanek "symmetric" prototype this means the bell is very broad at small gains and tightens as it is turned up — the opposite of a constant-Q digital bell, and a plausible description of why small 1073 mid boosts sound gentle.
- EQ1979 models the "12 kHz" shelf as a low-Q shelf centred at 2 kHz: the 1073 HF band evidently starts lifting from ~1–2 kHz and is labelled by where it approaches full boost, not by a midpoint. For a vocal "character" this suggests: Neve-style top = broad, early-starting shelf; Maag-style top = first-order shelf that starts late.
- "Proportional Q" (API) and the Pultec HF law are the same idea: a single damping resistance that the gain control and the bandwidth share. A defensible generic law for a "console" character, pending real measurements, is `Q(g) = Q0·(1 + c·|g|)` — the constants in dusk-audio's code (c ≈ 0.075–0.1 per dB) are tuning choices, not measurements.

### Gaps
- No open-source circuit-level (schematic-derived or WDF) model of the Neve 1073, API 550/560 or SSL E/G EQ was found; I found no published transfer functions for them either. The EQ1979 and dusk-audio constants are unverified fits/heuristics.
- The published WDF Pultec thesis/SMC paper were not opened (only the code/README); component values come from the code.
- The EQP-WDF-1A model contains no bump at the low shelf corner (the shelf rises monotonically to DC); real units roll off below ~20 Hz because of transformers, which the model omits. The popular "boost at 80 Hz, dip at 200 Hz" description for the 30 Hz setting (MusicTech text in the repo README) is therefore not reproduced literally by the model (model: shelf + dip at 282 Hz at max/max).
- API 550A/550B band tables and SSL frequency ranges/Q values: primary documents (apiaudio.com, UA manuals) were blocked; the figures above are incomplete and conflicting.
- No WDF or published model of a Baxandall beyond the BYOD component list was analysed numerically.

## 5. Click-free parameter changes, denormals, and numerical precision (f32 vs f64) at low frequencies

### Takeaway
Open EQs avoid clicks by smoothing *parameters* and recomputing coefficients often (fil4: per-sample linear coefficient ramps and ≤ 2x change per block; EQ1979: 10 Hz parameter low-pass with per-sample recompute) or by using structures that tolerate modulation (Simper SVF, Ivantsov's recursion, Fons Adriaensen's lattice). In f32, direct-form biquads are measurably noisy for low corners at high sample rates (−77 dBFS for a 30 Hz high-pass at 96 kHz, −62 dBFS for 20 Hz at 192 kHz in my test) whereas the trapezoidal SVF in f32 stays near −110 dBFS, so use the SVF (or f64 state) for the high-pass and low bands.

### Cited Findings
- fil4 parametric section: target coefficients are computed once per call and ramped linearly per sample (`s1 += d1; s2 += d2; a += da`); each of frequency, gain and bandwidth is limited to a factor of 2 change per call; the host loop calls it at intervals of at most 48 samples (comment in `hip.h`: "called an interval of max 48 samples"). [SRC] — [filters.h](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/filters.h), [hip.h](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/hip.h)
- fil4 shelves: parameters are one-pole smoothed with coefficient `lpf = 440/rate` per update, snapped to target when within 1e-4 (gain), 0.3 Hz (freq), 1e-3 (Q), and the RBJ coefficients are recomputed while changing. The high-pass fades in/out by interpolating its coefficients with a 0.01 per-step one-pole. [SRC] — [iir.h](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/iir.h), [hip.h](https://github.com/x42/fil4.lv2/blob/master/src/hip.h)
- EQ1979: gains pass through a 10 Hz one-pole low-pass (`fparam = 10`) and all band coefficients are recomputed every sample "for automation-safe operation". [SRC] — [bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx](https://github.com/D4p0up/eq1979/blob/main/bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx), [readme](https://github.com/D4p0up/eq1979/blob/main/readme.md)

### Inferences
- **Recommended real-time scheme for the Rust EQ**: (1) smooth the user parameters (frequency in log domain, gain in dB, Q) with a ~10–20 ms one-pole or linear ramp; (2) recompute coefficients every 16–32 samples in f64 (all matched formulas need a few `exp/cos/sqrt`, trivial at that rate) and linearly interpolate the coefficients per sample in between, or recompute per sample for SVF bands (one `tan`); (3) run the high-pass, low shelf and any band below ~1 kHz as a Simper SVF (pre-warped bilinear is exact enough there) and the upper bands as matched biquads; (4) keep filter state in f64 even if the audio path is f32 — at five bands mono the cost is irrelevant and it removes the low-frequency noise issue entirely; (5) alternatively convert the matched biquad to SVF form with the mapping in section 2 to get both decramping and SVF robustness.
- Interpolating direct-form *coefficients* is safe for the slow ramps of a channel EQ, but not guaranteed for fast modulation; that is the reason ZL and Ivantsov use dedicated structures. A vocal EQ with smoothed knobs and (optionally) a slow dynamic band does not need audio-rate modulation.
- Denormals in Rust: set FTZ/DAZ on the audio thread (x86 MXCSR; FPCR FZ on AArch64) at the start of the callback and additionally keep a cheap guard (state snap below ~1e-20, or a ±1e-20 alternating offset) for platforms where flags cannot be set (WASM has no FTZ, which matters if the same DSP runs in a web build).
- With f64 state the residual precision concern is only in *coefficient computation* of matched designs at very low fc/fs (cancellation in `1 + a1 + a2` and in Vicanek's `B` terms); following chowdsp, simply use the bilinear/SVF design below ~1 kHz, where the two are indistinguishable anyway (0.000 dB difference at 100 Hz in section 2's table).

### Gaps
- I did not measure zipper noise or transient artefacts of each structure under parameter modulation (only steady-state round-off noise), and I did not read Wishnick's DAFx paper or the Cytomic/Zavalishin texts on time-varying stability.
- KVR forum threads (mystran, Z1202/Zavalishin, Vicanek) on these topics were not readable from this session (HTTP 403); no forum claims are relied upon.
- The round-off figures are for one test signal and one filter type; treat them as order-of-magnitude.

## 6. Recommended minimal vocal EQ design (bands, ranges, curve types, 2–3 characters), with justification — including vocal EQ practice from engineering sources

### Takeaway
A six-control layout covers what credible vocal-EQ guidance asks for: high-pass (default ~80–90 Hz), low shelf, a low-mid bell for mud/box (default cut), a presence bell (small moves of 1–2 dB), a first-order "air" shelf with 10/20/40 kHz corners (boost-only), and a ±6 dB tilt; three characters — Clean (analog-matched, symmetric), Console (Neve-like fixed points, gain-dependent Q, 18 dB/oct high-pass) and Tube/Passive (Pultec low boost+attenuate biquad and coupled high boost) — can all be built from the minimum-phase sections documented above with zero latency.

### Cited Findings

**Vocal EQ practice**
- iZotope's vocal guide divides the voice into: unwanted low end 1–100 Hz; body/warmth 100–400 Hz ("The fundamental frequencies of a vocal are typically found between 100 and 400 Hz"); hollow/boxy 400–800 Hz; nasal 800 Hz–1.5 kHz; presence/intelligibility 1.5–5 kHz; sibilance 5–8 kHz; sparkle 8–12 kHz; air 12–20 kHz. [SRC] — [iZotope: How to EQ vocals](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/how-to-eq-vocals.html)
- High-pass: "engage a HPF … and remove everything from anywhere between 50–100 Hz and below"; method: start at 20 Hz, raise until it affects the vocal, back off; their example uses 90 Hz at 48 dB/octave. [SRC] — [iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/how-to-eq-vocals.html)
- Presence: ears are most sensitive at 2–5 kHz; "Making small boosts or cuts in this area, no more than 1 or 2 dB is common. Too much in this frequency range and the vocal quickly becomes harsh and aggressive." The author prefers a small cut around 3.6 kHz and brightening at 8–12 kHz instead. [SRC] — [iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/how-to-eq-vocals.html)
- Amounts used in that worked example: cuts at 185 Hz and 370 Hz (wide Q), a "very small cut" at 657 Hz, a cut at 931 Hz (nasal), about +1.5 dB at 9357 Hz with a fairly wide Q, about +3 dB high shelf from 12 kHz ("If the original recording is dark, I typically boost with a high shelf from 12 kHz and up. If … already fairly bright … from 16 kHz and up"). [SRC] — [iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/how-to-eq-vocals.html)
- Sibilance: most prominent 5–8 kHz; "Although sibilance can be softened by cutting frequencies using EQ, doing so can leave the rest of the vocal performance sounding dull and lifeless … better dealt with by manual editing … or by using a de-esser" (their de-esser starts at 4.4 kHz). [SRC] — [iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/how-to-eq-vocals.html)
- Sound On Sound (Mike Senior, de-essing): "sibilant noise bursts are usually focused somewhere in a region from 4-10kHz"; simplest detector set-up: high-pass the side-chain "at around 4kHz", optionally "a whopping peak EQ boost at about 7.5kHz"; most off-the-shelf de-essers are "a single band of dynamic EQ that can be targeted at the harshest sibilance region". [SRC] — [SOS: Techniques for vocal de-essing](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/techniques-vocal-de-essing)
- Sound On Sound ("Using EQ"): "it's usually better to apply EQ cut than EQ boost"; "try EQ cut to solve localised problems, using a bandwidth narrow enough … whereas EQ boost should be over wider bandwidths and using as little gain as will suffice"; for vocals: "Plosives and handling noise can be reduced by cutting below about 100Hz"; "The 7-12kHz region will emphasise sibilance and breath noise"; "The 16-18kHz region … gives a crispness to vocals that helps pull out details"; a lead vocal is brought forward by giving it more energy above ~10 kHz than the backing vocals. [SRC] — [SOS: Using EQ](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/using-eq)
- Other guides (search summary, lower authority): high-pass 80–100 Hz at 12–24 dB/oct; mud cut 2–4 dB at 200–400 Hz; presence +1 to +4 dB with Q 1–2; air shelf +1 to +3 dB at 10–12 kHz; presence 1–4 kHz and sibilance 5–10 kHz per Audient. [SEC] — [Audient: How to EQ vocals](https://audient.com/tutorial/how-to-eq-vocals/), [Sage Audio: Top 10 Vocal EQ Tips](https://www.sageaudio.com/blog/mastering/top-10-vocal-eq-tips)
- Hardware "character" reference points: Maag Air Band on close vocals "enhance[s] the breathiness and detail, but without adding any hint of harshness", and cutting the 2.5 kHz shelf while boosting the Air Band "formed a very powerful tool for taming any edge of harshness without losing the high-end sparkle"; Pultec at 60 Hz with boost and atten both at 2 "really helped to fill out the low end … without making it muddy", HF boost at 3 with 10 or 12 kHz "brought out a lovely high-end gloss". [SRC] — [SOS Maag EQ4M](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/maag-audio-eq4m), [SOS Pultec EQP-1A](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/pulse-techniques-eqp-1a)

**Building blocks already documented above (all zero-latency, minimum-phase)**
- Matched bell / HP / LP: Vicanek via [chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h](https://github.com/Chowdhury-DSP/chowdsp_utils/blob/master/modules/dsp/chowdsp_filters/Utils/chowdsp_CoefficientCalculators.h); matched shelves and tilt: [ivantsov-filters filters.h](https://github.com/yIvantsov/ivantsov-filters/blob/main/filters.h); SVF: [simper-filter](https://docs.rs/simper-filter/0.1.1/src/simper_filter/lib.rs.html); air band data: [Luftikus coeffcreator.h](https://github.com/lkjbdsp/lkjb-plugins/blob/master/Luftikus/Source/dsp/coeffcreator.h); Pultec values: [EQP1A.h](https://github.com/ABSounds/EQP-WDF-1A/blob/main/Source/EQP1A.h); Neve-style fit: [bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx](https://github.com/D4p0up/eq1979/blob/main/bellesondes_eq1979.jsfx).
- First-order tilt as implemented in ZL (study only): a first-order high shelf of gain `g` scaled by `1/√g`, i.e. `−g/2` dB below the pivot and `+g/2` dB above. — [ivantsov_coeff.cpp `get1TiltShelf`](https://github.com/ZL-Audio/ZLEqualizer/blob/main/source/dsp/filter/iir_filter/coeff/ivantsov_coeff.cpp)

### Inferences

**Recommended band layout (mono, 44.1/48/96 kHz, all sections minimum-phase IIR, state in f64)**

| # | Band | Range / default | Curve | Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High-pass | 30–300 Hz, default 80 Hz, on | 12 dB/oct Butterworth (Clean); 18 dB/oct = 1st-order + 2nd-order Q 1.0 (Console) | Simper SVF (pre-warped); bilinear is exact here |
| 2 | Low shelf ("Body") | 60–300 Hz, default 120 Hz, ±12 dB | 2nd-order shelf Q 0.707 (Clean); Pultec LF biquad with Boost + Atten (Tube) | SVF shelf, or the derived Pultec analog biquad → bilinear |
| 3 | Bell 1 ("Mud/Box") | 150 Hz–1.5 kHz, default 300 Hz, ±12 dB, Q 0.4–6, default Q 1.2 | Symmetric bell | SVF bell below 1 kHz, matched bell above |
| 4 | Bell 2 ("Presence") | 1–10 kHz, default 3.5 kHz, ±12 dB, Q 0.4–6, default Q 0.8 | Symmetric matched bell (Clean); gain-dependent Q (Console); Pultec HF-boost law (Tube) | Vicanek matched peaking |
| 5 | Air shelf | corner 5 / 10 / 20 / 40 kHz (or continuous 2.5–40 kHz), 0…+12 dB, boost-only | First-order shelf, DC gain 1 | Vicanek one-pole or Ivantsov first-order (uncorrected warp when fc ≥ ~0.45·fs) |
| 6 | Tilt | pivot 800 Hz (fixed), ±6 dB | First-order tilt | First-order matched shelf of gain g scaled by 1/√g |

- Why these six: bands 1, 3, 4, 5 map one-to-one onto the four things every cited guide does to a vocal (remove rumble below 80–100 Hz, cut 200–400 Hz mud or 400–800 Hz box, adjust 2–5 kHz presence by 1–2 dB, add air above 10–12 kHz); the low shelf restores body after a high-pass and hosts the Pultec trick; the tilt is a one-knob "darker/brighter" correction for a mismatched microphone. A dedicated sibilance band is deliberately absent because both iZotope and SOS say sibilance is better handled by a de-esser/dynamic band than by static EQ — the EQ should expose a hook (side-chain of bell 2, or a separate de-esser module) rather than a static 5–8 kHz cut.
- Why the air shelf is first-order and boost-only with corners up to 40 kHz: that is the Maag/Luftikus behaviour (section 3) and the reason it adds "air" without lifting 5–8 kHz sibilance much — e.g. Luftikus' 40 kHz setting at half gain is +1.0 dB at 5 kHz but +3.3 dB at 20 kHz. A matched one-pole shelf reproduces this at 48 kHz within ~0.2–0.5 dB.
- Gain ranges of ±12 dB are generous: the practice sources work with 1–3 dB boosts and 2–4 dB cuts; consider a fine default knob law (e.g. ±6 dB over the central 70% of travel).

**Three characters (selectable; all share the same six controls)**
1. **Clean (modern, transparent)** — RBJ analog prototypes realised with matched designs so curves are identical at 44.1/48/96 kHz and do not cramp: symmetric boost/cut bells (bandwidth `fc/Q` at half gain), Butterworth high-pass, 2nd-order shelves with Q 0.707, first-order air and tilt. No saturation.
2. **Console (Neve 1073-flavoured)** — high-pass 18 dB/oct with a small resonance bump at the corner (EQ1979 fits +1 to +2.4 dB, Q 1.2–1.9 just above the corner); low band as a very broad low-Q bell/shelf; bell Q that rises with |gain| (`Q ≈ 0.22 + |g|/60` in EQ1979's fit, steeper in the top positions) and optionally frequency positions snapped to 360/700/1.6k/3.2k/4.8k/7.2k Hz; top band as a low-Q (≈0.35) broad shelf that starts lifting around 2 kHz instead of the late first-order air shelf. Optional gentle arctan-type saturation before the EQ. All constants are that open project's fit — present it as "console-style", not as a verified 1073 model.
3. **Tube / Passive (Pultec-flavoured)** — low shelf control becomes *two* amounts (Boost and Atten) feeding the derived LF biquad with the 20/30/60/100 Hz capacitor sets (for vocals 60 and 100 Hz are the useful ones: at 100 Hz, max/max gives a shelf plus a −6.5 dB dip at ~940 Hz; moderate settings give "+3 dB shelf, −2 dB dip" per SOS); bell 2 uses the Pultec HF-boost law (Qp, Qz from bandwidth and boost; +10 dB broad to +20 dB sharp at full boost, frequencies 3/4/5/8/10/12/16 kHz); air shelf replaced or complemented by the first-order HF attenuate section for taming harshness.
- An "Air" voicing could be offered as a fourth option or folded into Clean: Luftikus-style fixed bands (10/40/160/650 Hz broad Q≈0.4 bells + 2.5 kHz shelf + air) in a parallel topology. It is simple (fixed tables or one-pole sections) and MIT-licensed data exists, but its parallel gain interaction (+4 dB broadband at full boost on the hardware) makes it less predictable in a chain; a series implementation with unity DC gain is the safer default.

**Implementation order suggestion**: (1) Simper SVF + parameter smoothing + f64 state (covers bands 1–3 and Clean immediately); (2) Vicanek matched bell and one-pole air/tilt shelves with unit tests that compare against the analog prototype at 44.1/48/96 kHz (targets: ≤ 0.35 dB for bells with Q ≥ 0.7 below 12 kHz, ≤ 0.3 dB for the first-order air shelf — the numbers measured in sections 2 and 3); (3) Pultec LF/HF closed forms; (4) Console voicing constants behind a clearly labelled "inspired by" character.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies "how much gain is typical" across many professional mixes; the amounts above are from individual tutorials/reviews (one iZotope worked example, SOS editorial advice), not from a survey.
- Dynamic-EQ behaviour for harshness (attack/release, thresholds, band Q) was not researched here beyond the SOS de-essing description; it is presumably covered by the de-esser/dynamics notes of the parallel research tasks.
- The Console and Tube character constants rest on one open curve-fit (EQ1979) and one open circuit model (EQP-WDF-1A); neither was validated against hardware measurements by me. Listening tests and comparison with published response plots (SOS has Audio Precision plots for the 1073N, EQP-1A and EQ4M) should precede shipping them under a hardware-referencing name.
- Whether the product should imitate hardware names at all (trademark considerations for "Pultec", "Neve", "Maag", "Air Band") was not examined.
