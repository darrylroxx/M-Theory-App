## 1. Cross-modal correspondences between hearing and vision: direction, strength, robustness

### Takeaway
The best-supported links are pitch to lightness (and height and small size), loudness to size and visual salience, harsh/rough/bright timbre to angular, spiky shapes, and whole-music mood to colour (fast/major to lighter, more saturated, yellower; slow/minor to darker, greyer, bluer), the last being almost entirely mediated by emotion. Pitch to hue, pitch class to hue, register to warm/cool, attack to edge sharpness and staccato to dots have little or no controlled evidence and should be treated as design choices, not facts.

### Cited Findings

Pitch
- [R] A tutorial review states that "people consistently match high-pitched sounds with small, bright objects that are located high up in space", treats such correspondences as shared by large numbers of people and possibly universal, and cautions that pitch–size may simply reflect redundant coding of object size. — [Spence 2011, Atten Percept Psychophys 73:971–995](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-010-0073-7)
- [R] In 22 implicit-association experiments, pitch was associated specifically with colour **lightness**; shifting spectral energy upward at fixed pitch (higher spectral centroid) was linked to higher luminance and saturation, like higher pitch; **high saturation** went with greater loudness, higher pitch and higher spectral centroid; a trill was weakly associated with low saturation; **hue** (at matched luminance and saturation) was not associated with any acoustic feature except a weak preference for higher pitch with blue over yellow. — [Anikin & Johansson 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6407832/)
- [R] Lower-pitched music makes visual objects be judged darker than higher-pitched music at constant luminance. — [Psychology of Music 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03057356231216950)
- [R] When people drew on a tablet while listening, most represented pitch with height and loudness with line thickness; pitch–height was drawn more accurately than loudness–thickness; untrained participants used more diverse strategies and ignored pitch that did not change; in related free drawings loudness was mostly shown as size. — [Küssner & Leech-Wilkinson](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/investigating-the-influence-of-musical-training-on-cross-modal-co/); [Front Psychol 5:789](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00789/full)
- [R] Pitch–height is not settled as universal. Speakers of a Cambodian hill language with no height metaphor for pitch still showed height–pitch associations, and prelinguistic infants looked longer at congruent stimuli for both height–pitch and thickness–pitch; but Farsi speakers' pitch judgements were modulated by thickness and not height, a 2022 study found no height–pitch association in Farsi speakers on a non-linguistic task, Turkish-speaking children reversed it, and it was unreliable in Dutch children before age 11. — [Dolscheid et al. 2014](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_1936459_6/component/file_2056418/Dolscheid_etal_pss_2014.pdf); [Cognition 2020 manuscript](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/158204/1/Pitpitch_Cognition_Final.pdf); [Oxford record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:de15b129-68c2-4083-85ee-2a0bbbc22c69)
- [R] In one timbre study that also varied fundamental frequency, fundamental frequency showed no link to height, greyscale or colour (task with 12 fixed shapes). — [Adeli et al. 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00352/full); contradicted by the lightness findings of [Anikin & Johansson 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6407832/)

Loudness
- [R] A crossmodal correspondence between auditory and visual intensity (louder = brighter) was shown in speeded classification and has been replicated; very young infants are reported to respond to brightness–loudness equivalence; children match high pitch with small size by age 3 (bouncing balls) and 9 (matching task). — [review in PMC5835347](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5835347); [PeerJ 4443](https://peerj.com/articles/4443.pdf); [Walker et al. 2010](https://www.babylab.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2016/09/Walker_etal2010.pdf)
- [R] Loudness mapped onto greater visual **saliency** rather than luminance as such. — [Anikin & Johansson 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6407832/)
- [R] When 22 participants set the colour and the **size** of a patch to 27 film-music excerpts, angry music got large red patches and sad music smaller patches toward dark blue. — [Lindborg & Friberg 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4671663/)

Timbre brightness, sharpness of shape (bouba/kiki)
- [R] 23 musical sounds at fixed loudness were matched to 12 coloured or greyscale shapes: soft timbres went with blue, green or light-grey **rounded** shapes, harsh timbres with red, yellow or dark-grey **sharp angular** shapes; the timbre–shape link was strong and unaffected by colour vs greyscale; greyscale choices were at chance for 7 of 23 sounds. A 2021 replication found the harsh/jagged and smooth/soft pairings "associated with a high level of consistency". — [Adeli, Rouat & Molotchnikoff 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00352/full); [replication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35065536/)
- [R] The bouba/kiki effect held across 917 speakers of 25 languages in 9 language families and 10 writing systems, and was not stronger for scripts with rounder or spikier letterforms. — [Ćwiek et al. 2022](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/bitstream/2445/183962/1/719545.pdf); [press summary](https://leibniz-zas.de/en/about-us/news/details/news/seeing-and-hearing-in-conjunction-what-bouba-and-kiki-reveal-about-language-evolution)
- [R] Timbral brightness is usually modelled as spectral centroid; incongruent timbral-brightness and pitch-height pairs slow responses, and visual brightness interferes with timbral brightness judgements. — [Saitis & Wallmark 2024](https://comma.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/assets/pdf/s13414-024-02934-2.pdf)
- [R] For instrument timbres, semantic ratings predicted the **lightness and saturation** of the colours people matched to them; the effect was larger when register also varied but survived with register held constant; ratings of "warm/cool" timbre related only weakly to the warmth of chosen colours, and only in one experiment. — [Reymore & Lindsey 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11747214/)

Roughness and dissonance
- [R] A concert audience shaping a graphic while hearing drones of controlled roughness made spikier shapes for rougher sounds, replicated in the lab; square-wave tones are matched to angular shapes more than sine tones. — [Liew et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00178/full)
- [R] Sounds with sharp transients relate to rougher textures and round sounds to smoother textures. — [review citing Etzi et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1323873/pdf)
- [R] Rough sandpaper and dissonant harmonisations share an affective profile (high arousal, negative valence), suggesting the dissonance–roughness link is emotion-mediated (touch, not vision). — search summary of a 2025 Music Perception study returned with [Liew et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00178/full)

Attack, articulation, tempo
- [R] No study isolating attack time against edge sharpness was found. Closest: ratings of auditory roundness/spikiness correlate with spectral tilt, the temporal FFT and the amplitude envelope. — [Lacey et al. 2020](https://scholarblogs.emory.edu/nygaardlab/files/2024/10/Lacey-et-al-Cog-Sci-2020.pdf); abruptness of the amplitude envelope is named as a guiding feature in [a correspondences review](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/76988/1/Cross_Sensory_Correspondences.pdf)
- [R] Legato melodies were rated more cohesive, calmer and sadder; staccato melodies as having more tension and energy. — [Macquarie University study 2023](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-perceptual-and-emotional-consequences-of-articulation-in-musi/)
- [T] Only informal classroom drawings were found for staccato vs legato shapes (legato drawn as long weaving wave shapes, staccato as triangles and zigzags). — [course blog](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/rosenspevackfylcf18/?p=1002)
- [R] Increasing tempo led to increasing hand speed in the drawing paradigm, but not when loudness was falling at the same time. — [Front Psychol 5:789](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00789/full)

Whole-music colour via emotion (mode, tempo, loudness)
- [R] In US and Mexican samples, faster major-mode music led to lighter, more saturated, yellower colour choices and slower minor-mode music to darker, desaturated, bluer ones; correlations between emotional ratings of the music and of the chosen colours were 0.89 < r < 0.99; evidence is correlational. — [Palmer, Schloss, Xu & Prado-León 2013, PNAS 110(22)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1212562110)
- [R] Across 34 excerpts from many genres, loud, punchy, distorted music went with **darker, redder, more saturated** colours; partialling out emotion removed all significant low-level cross-modal correlations; two latent factors (arousal, valence) mediated the links. — [Whiteford, Schloss, Helwig & Palmer 2018, i-Perception 9(6)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6240980)
- [R] Happy music was associated with yellow, anger with large red patches, sadness with small dark-blue patches; models including emotion explained 60–75% of the variation in each colour-patch parameter (cross-validated R²). — [Lindborg & Friberg 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4671663/)
- [R] Colour–emotion associations are highly similar across 30 nations (4,598 participants, 22 languages; mean pattern similarity r = .88), with some national variation. — [Jonauskaite et al. 2020](https://biopen.bi.no/bi-xmlui/bitstream/11250/2761614/1/Jonauskaite_etal_2020_PsychScience.pdf)

Register to warm/cool, pitch to hue
- [R] There is "still no consensus" on pitch–hue: the implicit-association work found only weak high-pitch–blue, while a binary-choice study reported yellow–high and blue–low and an older child study matched high pitch to yellow. — [Anikin & Johansson 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6407832/); [NTNU thesis record](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/3092858?show=full); [PeerJ 4443](https://peerj.com/articles/4443)

### Inferences
Evidence grade used below: A = replicated and/or cross-cultural; B = several studies, some caveats; C = single study or indirect; D = no controlled evidence found (pure design choice).

| Sound property (how to measure) | Paint property | Direction | Grade | Suggested strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pitch / frequency (already the canvas Y axis) | Vertical position | higher = higher | B (A in Western listeners; contested cross-culturally) | fixed by the app |
| Pitch / frequency | Lightness of the mark | higher = lighter | A | moderate: a lightness ramp over the Y axis, so the top of the canvas is lighter than the bottom even at equal loudness |
| Pitch / frequency | Mark size (dab diameter, stroke width) | higher = smaller, lower = bigger and heavier | B | moderate: bass marks 2–4× the width of top-register marks |
| Loudness (RMS or per-band magnitude, relative to the track's own running level) | Size / width / pressure | louder = bigger, thicker | A | strong; make it the primary driver of width |
| Loudness | Salience: contrast against the ground, opacity, paint load | louder = more contrast and more paint | B | strong |
| Loudness | Saturation | louder = more saturated | B | moderate |
| Timbre brightness (spectral centroid, ideally relative to the fundamental so it is not just pitch again) | Lightness and saturation | brighter timbre = lighter, more saturated | B | moderate |
| Harshness (spectral centroid + roughness; square-like or distorted spectra) | Angularity of the mark: bristle splay, ragged edge, straight segments and corners in the path | harsher = more angular/spiky; soft = round, smooth-edged | A (shape), C (as brush texture) | strong |
| Roughness / dissonance (sensory roughness, spectral flatness, inharmonicity) | Surface texture: dry-brush break-up, grain, scatter | rougher = more broken and spiky | B | strong |
| Roughness | Saturation | rougher (trill-like) = slightly less saturated | C | weak |
| Attack sharpness (log attack time, onset strength) | Edge hardness at the start of the mark; spatter | sharper attack = harder, crisper leading edge; slow attack = feathered fade-in | D (indirect C) | design choice; keep but tune by eye |
| Duration (note or partial sustain) | Touch vs pulled stroke | short = dab, held = stroke whose length follows duration | D for the visual claim, but it is the direct spatial analogue of time | fixed by the app |
| Articulation (gap between successive events, staccato ratio) | Separate dabs vs connected line; reload between marks | staccato = separate, crisp, re-loaded dabs; legato = one continuous, depleting stroke | D (emotion evidence only: staccato = energy/tension, legato = calm) | design choice |
| Tempo / onset density | Gesture speed, number of marks, direction variety | faster/denser = faster strokes (drier, more skipping), more marks, more direction changes | C | moderate; let loudness override when the two disagree, as in the drawing data |
| Mode, tempo and loudness together (mood) | Palette: hue family, lightness, saturation | fast + major = lighter, more saturated, yellower; slow + minor = darker, greyer, bluer; loud + distorted = darker, redder, saturated | A within the cultures tested, emotion-mediated | strong, but apply slowly at section level, not per note |
| Register | Warm vs cool | none established | D | do not hard-wire; if wanted, treat as a style option |
| Pitch class / key | Hue | none established for non-synaesthetes | D | style option only (see section 7) |

- Because the canvas already spends its vertical axis on pitch, the redundant pitch cues (lightness, size) should be gentle ramps; the free channels (hue, saturation, wetness, texture) are better spent on the mood and timbre links, which are the ones listeners actually agree on.
- Hue should be driven by mood and harmony, not by pitch: the research gives hue no reliable acoustic partner, while giving lightness and saturation several.
- Robust across people: pitch–lightness, loudness–size/salience, harsh–angular (25 languages), and music–colour via emotion (two cultures, plus 30-nation agreement on colour–emotion). Not robust: any absolute hue code.

### Gaps
- Full texts could not be opened, so effect sizes other than those quoted (r = .89–.99; 60–75% R²; 917 speakers; r = .88) are missing, including the per-shape percentages in the timbre–shape study and the exact colour set of the PNAS study.
- No controlled study was found for attack time to edge sharpness, staccato/legato to dots/lines, note density to visual density, or register to warm/cool colour.
- The tutorial review's own table of correspondences and evidence ratings could not be retrieved.
- The cross-cultural claim for pitch–height rests on abstracts that partly contradict each other; one abstract snippet was internally inconsistent.

## 2. Mood models: valence/arousal from audio features to palette, stroke energy and wetness

### Takeaway
Arousal can be estimated fairly well from loudness, spectral brightness and tempo/onset density and should drive saturation, contrast and stroke energy; valence is much harder to estimate from audio and should be applied cautiously, mainly through mode and consonance, to lightness and the yellow–blue axis. No published or open-source mapping from mood to paint wetness was found; that part is inference.

### Cited Findings
- [R] Six cues manipulated factorially over 200 examples ranked in importance as mode > tempo > register > dynamics > articulation > timbre (ranking varies by emotion); cues contributed mostly **linearly** (77–89% of variance) and **additively** (interactions negligible). — [Eerola, Friberg & Bresin 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00487/full)
- [R] Fast tempo, high loudness and high pitch go with high-arousal emotions (happiness, anger); slow tempo, legato articulation and soft timbre with low-arousal emotions (sadness, calm). — [same paper, PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3726864)
- [R] In music-emotion recognition, arousal is the more reliably predicted dimension (RMS energy, spectral centroid, zero-crossing rate, tempo); valence has no comparably clear acoustic correlate and is usually tied to mode, consonance/dissonance, pulse clarity and articulation; one regression system reached R² ≈ 0.63 for arousal against 0.356 for valence on 194 clips. — [feature-selection review, arXiv 2212.13369](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13369); [SciTePress 2016](https://scitepress.org/Papers/2016/58265/pdf/index.html); [CAIT 2025](https://cs.uef.fi/sipu/pub/CAIT-2025.pdf)
- [R] Colour to emotion: Pleasure = 0.69·Brightness + 0.22·Saturation; Arousal = −0.31·Brightness + 0.60·Saturation; Dominance = −0.76·Brightness + 0.32·Saturation (standardised variables). — [Valdez & Mehrabian 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/)
- [R] One visualisation paper inverted those equations to Brightness = 1.26·v − 0.46·a and Saturation = 0.64·v + 1.43·a (its own derivation). — [Emosaic, arXiv 2002.10096](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.10096)
- [R] A dataset of 2,634 expert-validated music–palette pairs aligned through valence–arousal vectors was built on the argument that a single dominant colour per track misses emotional variation; the model outputs multi-colour palettes and is trained for colour accuracy, palette diversity and emotion consistency. — [Music2Palette, arXiv 2507.04758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04758)
- [R] Music and lighting rated similarly for valence and arousal were judged to match better when presented together. — [Hauck et al. 2022](https://experimental.psychologie.uni-mainz.de/files/2025/01/2022hauck-crossmodal-color.pdf)
- [C] An MIT-licensed prototype estimates `arousal = 0.5·loudness + 0.4·density + 0.1·onset`; valence from the share of mid+high band "exertion" (each band divided by its own running average), plus 0.15 on strong onsets; smoothing speed rises with energy, `glide = clamp(0.05 + 0.15·arousal + 0.2·onset, 0.02, 0.6)`. Hue (HSV 0–1) is a blend of a high-arousal arc `0.95 + 0.21·v` (crimson to red-orange to yellow) and a low-arousal arc `0.70 − 0.30·v` (indigo to cyan to green), blended circularly by arousal; saturation = `0.4 + 0.6·arousal`; value follows loudness and transients; the key adds a tint of at most ±0.06 hue, inverted for minor keys; when smoothed arousal exceeds 0.70 the colour is held 95% to the slow palette and 5% to the instant value to stop flicker (otherwise 70/30); 120 ms flash cooldown. Its README says the values "are not validated measurements of a listener's emotion". — [music-reactive-lighting, app/mapping/emotion.py and color.py](https://github.com/CanYuzbey/music-reactive-lighting)
- [C] A JavaScript library (MIT) maps energy to size, spectral flatness ("roughness") to noise displacement of the shape (`1 + roughness·3`), spectral centroid ("warmth") to hue, perceptual sharpness to luminance, pitch class to rainbow hue and octave to lightness; its paper's study was qualitative (8 participants), with composers saying timbre shown as texture matched their intuition. — [musicolors source](https://github.com/ChungHaLee/musicolors); [arXiv 2503.14220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14220)
- [T] Quick strokes read as energy and movement; hard pressure gives thick bold strokes with visible texture, light pressure fine lines; faster strokes skip over the surface texture leaving broken marks while slower strokes let paint settle into the tooth. — [painting-school guide](https://willkempartschool.com/how-to-paint-looser-mastering-brushwork-techniques-for-impressonistic-art/); [watercolour note](https://chidanandm.substack.com/p/understanding-brush-strokes-thin)
- [T] A museum glossary notes that brushmarks and paint texture can themselves convey feeling, "a kind of handwriting", and defines gestural painting as paint applied in free sweeping gestures in which the viewer reads the maker's emotion. — [impasto](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/i/impasto); [gestural](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/g/gestural)

### Inferences
- Trust ordering: arousal estimates (R² about 0.6) deserve strong, fast mappings; valence estimates (R² about 0.35) deserve weak, slow ones. Compute both over windows of several seconds and smooth more when energy is low, as the open-source prototype does.
- Because cues combine linearly and additively, a weighted sum is a defensible model: arousal ≈ w1·loudness + w2·onset density + w3·spectral centroid + w4·tempo; valence ≈ w5·major-ness (chroma match to major vs minor templates) + w6·consonance (inverse roughness) + small tempo term.
- Palette from mood, consistent with section 1 and the colour-emotion equations: saturation rises with arousal; lightness rises with valence and falls slightly with arousal; hue leans yellow/warm for positive valence and blue for negative valence at low arousal, and toward red with darker value for negative valence at high arousal; contrast (value range and edge hardness) rises with arousal.
- Wetness, a proposal with no direct source: map (1 − arousal) to dilution and wet-on-wet behaviour. Calm music: diluted paint, long open time, soft edges, slow wide strokes, blooms allowed, rounded marks. Aggressive music (high arousal, low valence, high roughness): thick or dry paint, hard edges, fast strokes that skip and split, impasto ridges, spatter on sharp onsets, angular paths. The supporting chain is indirect: soft timbres pair with rounded smooth shapes and harsh ones with angular shapes; roughness pairs with spikiness and with high-arousal negative affect; legato reads as calmer and staccato as tenser; quick, hard strokes read as energetic.
- Hold the palette at section level and let individual notes vary only inside it (the 95/5 lock above is a working example); this also protects against colour flicker and mud.

### Gaps
- No validated, published valence/arousal to brush-parameter mapping exists that I could find; wetness, edge hardness and spatter rules are untested design hypotheses.
- The exact per-emotion cue table from the classic speech/music meta-analysis could not be retrieved.
- Arousal/valence factor loadings on specific colour dimensions in the 2018 i-Perception study were not confirmed beyond "loud, punchy, distorted = darker, redder, more saturated".

## 3. Painters' marks as physical variables a simulation can set, and the expressive quality each carries

### Takeaway
Teaching sources and open brush engines converge on a small set of controllable variables: paint load, dilution, pressure, speed, angle/direction, bristle stiffness and splay, spacing of dabs, pickup of underlying paint, and whether the surface below is wet or dry. Each named technique is a specific setting of these.

### Cited Findings
- [T] Scumbling: thin, opaque, lighter paint dragged with a nearly dry brush over a darker dry layer so the underlayer shows through; broken, hazy result. Glazing: thin transparent layer over a dry opaque layer; shifts hue and luminosity; cool glazes recede and warm ones advance. Impasto: thick paint standing up from the surface, by knife or brush. Sgraffito: scratching through a layer (usually wet) to reveal the colour beneath; sharp, defined lines. Dry brush: stiff brush with little paint dragged lightly so specks catch on the peaks of the surface. Wet-on-wet (alla prima): paint into undried paint; edges blend and brushwork stays spontaneous. — [Milan Art Institute](https://www.milanartinstitute.com/blog/painting-techniques); [Draw Paint Academy](https://drawpaintacademy.com/9-oil-painting-techniques-for-beginners/); [University of Delaware](https://www1.udel.edu/artfoundations/beg_painting/techniques.html); [University of Evansville glossary](https://faculty.evansville.edu/rl29/art340/f04/paintingterms.html)
- [T] Sources disagree on two details: scumbling is usually dry-brush over dry paint but one source describes rubbing off a wet layer; sgraffito is usually into wet paint but some apply it to dry layers. — [Dummies](https://www.dummies.com/art-center/performing-arts/art-painting/exploring-glazing-in-oil-paintings/) vs [Draw Paint Academy](https://drawpaintacademy.com/9-oil-painting-techniques-for-beginners/)
- [T] Watercolour: wet-on-wet lets pigment spread with soft fading edges; wet-on-dry gives crisp shapes that stay inside the brush mark; blooms (backruns, "cauliflowers") form when wetter paint meets a damp, partly dried area and cannot be undone; dry brush uses very little water and leaves broken streaks on the paper texture; lifting removes wet paint by blotting; granulation is pigment settling into the paper texture; a final stroke of mostly water makes a wash fade out. — [Getty](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/prismatic/watercolor_techniques.html); [Watercolor Affair](https://www.watercoloraffair.com/?p=884); [watercolorpainting.com](https://watercolorpainting.com/13-easy-watercolor-techniques-tips-trick/); [Mont Marte](https://www.montmarte.com.au/blogs/tips-techniques/watercolour-techniques)
- [T] Stiff hog bristle grabs more paint, suits thick paint and accentuates brushstrokes; soft hair (sable and similar) suits thinned paint, smooth supports, control and fine blending. — [Blick fibre guide](https://www.dickblick.com/learning-resources/buying-guides/brush-fiber-differences/); [Mont Marte brush guide](https://www.montmarte.com/blogs/guide/guide-to-oil-paint-brushes); [Natural Pigments](https://www.naturalpigments.com/brushes-tools.html)
- [T] Pressure controls whether bristles spread into a wide flat mark or stay together for a narrow line; faster strokes skip over texture, slower ones fill it. — [painting-school guide](https://willkempartschool.com/how-to-paint-looser-mastering-brushwork-techniques-for-impressonistic-art/)
- [C] An open brush library (ISC licence) exposes the same variables as numbers. Inputs: pressure, fine speed, gross speed, random, stroke progress (0 to 1 along the stroke), direction, tilt, attack angle. Settings (min/default/max): opaque 0/1/2; hardness 0/0.8/1; dabs_per_actual_radius 0/2/200; dabs_per_second; radius_by_random 0/0/1.5; offset_by_random (dab position jitter, 1.0 = standard deviation of one radius); offset_by_speed; slow_tracking 0/0/10 (lag that removes jitter); tracking_noise 0/0/12; smudge 0/0/1 (paint with colour picked up from the canvas); smudge_length 0/0.5/1 (how quickly the picked-up colour updates); paint_mode 0–1 (spectral subtractive mixing); per-dab colour shifts change_color_h, _l, _hsl_s, _v; elliptical_dab_ratio 1–10 and angle; stroke_duration and stroke_holdtime; direction_filter; lock_alpha; eraser. — [libmypaint brushsettings.json](https://github.com/mypaint/libmypaint)
- [C] p5.brush (MIT, 1,430 stars) defines a brush by weight, scatter (sideways wobble), sharpness, grain, opacity (0–255), spacing (gap between tip stamps, 1 = no overlap), a pressure curve, tip type (default, spray, marker, custom, image), tip rotation (none, natural, random) and noise (per-stroke opacity variation). Built-in values [weight, scatter, sharpness, grain, opacity, spacing]: pen [0.3, 0.15, 0.9, 0.7, 150, 0.1]; soft pencil [0.3, 0.75, 0.45, 0.8, 180, 0.1]; charcoal [0.35, 1.5, 0.68, 2, 120, 0.03]; pastel [0.7, 5, 0.91, 1, 30, 0.028]; crayon [0.33, 1.9, 0.75, 2, 159, 0.07]; spray [0.2, 6, 15, 40, 90, 0.5]; marker [2, 0.2, –, –, 1, 0.03]. Watercolour fill defaults: bleed strength 0.07, texture strength 0.8; a fill whose edge touches a still-wet fill "loses its edge there and feathers into it", and the paper dries at each render. — [p5.brush src/stroke/stroke.js, src/fill/fill.js, README](https://github.com/acamposuribe/p5.brush)
- [C] A bristle-level oil simulator (MIT, 48 stars): bristle length up to 15 and thickness up to 5, horizontal bristle noise up to 4 and vertical noise 8; each bristle varies in brightness by ±9% (`BRIGHTNESS_RELATIVE_CHANGE 0.09`) so hair streaks show; from step 5 of a stroke the brush picks up the colour already on the canvas at `MIX_STRENGTH 0.012` per step; minimum alpha 20; stroke length 2.3 × brush size (minimum 16); brush position averaged over the last 4 points. — [ofxOilPaint src](https://github.com/jagracar/ofxOilPaint)
- [C] A stroke post-process gives each stroke a thickness profile of "a flat core, a rim, a scoop in the middle and bristle grooves along its length, with a per-stroke load"; thickness sums where strokes overlap and is lit by a lamp fixed to the canvas; the canvas weave shows only where paint is thin. (Licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: ideas only.) — [Unstill-Life architecture guide](https://github.com/RichardPieters/Unstill-Life)
- [C] Natural-media engines list the same physical processes as separable modules: water and pigment transport, evaporation, deposition, re-wetting, edge darkening, granulation, drips under gravity/tilt, absorbent paper height fields, Kubelka–Munk pigment mixing, a dual wet/dry layer and a settable drying rate. — [wetpaint README](https://github.com/bgyss/wetpaint); [efx-physic-paint README](https://github.com/electroheadfx/efx-physic-paint)

### Inferences
Variable settings per technique, and the expressive reading used in section 2 (the readings combine the teaching statements above with the research in section 1; they are not measured):

| Technique | Load | Dilution | Pressure / speed | Under-layer | Edge | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wash | high | very dilute | light, slow, wide | dry or damp | soft | calm, open, airy |
| Wet-on-wet, bloom | medium | dilute, wetter than the surface | light | wet | diffused, feathered, backruns | tender, dreamy, unstable |
| Glaze | low | transparent | light, even | dry | soft | depth, warmth or coolness shift, quiet |
| Scumble | low | thick but scant, opaque | light, scrubbing | dry | broken, hazy | veiled, misty, restrained |
| Dry brush | very low | undiluted | light and fast | dry | broken streaks | rough, brittle, energetic |
| Impasto | very high | undiluted | firm | any | ridged, hard | loud, forceful, physical |
| Sgraffito | removes paint | – | hard point | wet | sharp incised line | tense, cutting, percussive |
| Spatter / drips | high | dilute | flick (impulse) or gravity | any | hard dots, runs | explosive, accidental, raw |
| Connected pulled stroke | depletes along its length | medium | steady | any | fades as load runs out | sustained, sung |
| Separate dabs | re-loaded each time | medium | press and lift | any | crisp start, round | detached, rhythmic |

- A minimal simulation state per mark is therefore: load, dilution, pressure(t), speed(t), direction(t), bristle stiffness (splay per unit pressure), dab spacing, pickup rate, plus the wetness of the surface underneath. Every sound property in section 1 can be routed to one of these.
- Depletion along the stroke (load falling, grain rising) gives held notes a natural "decay" and visually matches a note's amplitude envelope without extra rules.

### Gaps
- Wikipedia, museum conservation pages and open painting textbooks were not reachable; definitions come from teaching sites and one museum glossary via search summaries.
- No source gave numeric water-to-pigment ratios, nor a textbook treatment of paint load and bristle stiffness; nothing authoritative was retrieved on drips, spatter or "sinking in" of oil paint.
- The expressive readings are conventional and inferred, not experimentally established.

## 4. Open-source projects that paint or draw from audio

### Takeaway
Few open projects truly paint from audio, and those that exist are small; almost all map only loudness and spectrum to size and colour. None found maps duration to stroke length, articulation to mark type, or mood to wetness, so the app's intended mapping is not already available off the shelf. The strongest reusable pieces are a brush library, a handful of mapping ideas, and generative-painting rule sets.

### Cited Findings
- [C] GitHub repository searches for "audio reactive painting", "music painting generative", "sound painting webgl", "audio watercolor", "spectrogram painting" and "generative art audio p5" returned 4, 4, 1, 14, 4 and 6 repositories respectively, almost all with fewer than 20 stars. — [GitHub search API](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=audio+reactive+painting)
- [C] watercolor-audio (no licence file): WebGL2 stable-fluids watercolour. A 12-bin chromagram drives pigment; pitch class maps to hue around the **circle of fifths** (`[0,7,2,9,4,11,6,1,8,3,10,5]`, HSV s 0.72, v 0.95) "so harmonically related notes get neighbouring hues"; each chroma bin above 0.22 injects pigment with intensity `c·(0.18 + 0.5·drive)` and radius `0.00008 + 0.00018·c` plus a swirl; onsets bloom the chord-root colour; placement follows a golden-angle spiral (`r = sqrt((k+0.5)/72)·0.42`) so blooms "scatter evenly and organically instead of clustering"; the layout preset swaps every 15 s; the dye field stores light absorption so display = paper − absorption (subtractive mixing) with wet-edge darkening and paper grain; `dyeDissipation 0.992`, `velocityDissipation 0.985`, `curl 24`. — [komei222/watercolor-audio js/main.js, js/fluid.js](https://github.com/komei222/watercolor-audio)
- [C] Aquarelle (MIT, 14 stars): single-file canvas visualiser with eight brush modes. The watercolour look is a two-pass stroke: a wide halo at alpha 0.10–0.13 (20–30 px) under a core at alpha 0.34–0.45 (7–13 px); bands are pooled with a power-law bin map (`pow(b/NB, 1.7)`) and smoothed by 0.34 per frame; energy is the mean of the lowest 10 bands; droplet count = `min(3, round(energy·5))`, speed scaled by `0.5 + energy·2`. Only loudness and spectrum are used. — [YT0383/Aquarelle-Visual-audio index.html](https://github.com/YT0383/Aquarelle-Visual-audio)
- [C] musicolors (MIT, 13 stars): energy to size, spectral flatness to noise distortion, spectral centroid to hue, perceptual sharpness to luminance, pitch class to rainbow hue, octave to lightness. — [ChungHaLee/musicolors](https://github.com/ChungHaLee/musicolors)
- [C] music-reactive-lighting (MIT): the arousal/valence to hue/saturation/value mapping detailed in section 2, with harmonic/percussive separation and key estimation from chroma. — [CanYuzbey/music-reactive-lighting](https://github.com/CanYuzbey/music-reactive-lighting)
- [C] Unstill-Life (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): microphone loudness plus spectral flux (rise of the 100–4000 Hz band every 46 ms) set a "restlessness"; in silence a painting grows stroke by stroke over the frame. Its stroke system is described in section 5. — [RichardPieters/Unstill-Life](https://github.com/RichardPieters/Unstill-Life)
- [C] paint-mv-skills (MIT, 76 stars): turns a song and lyrics into a hand-painted watercolour video with p5.brush. Every frame is a pure function of song time; the onset envelope is spectral flux over 40 log-spaced bands from 40 Hz to 10 kHz in dB (positive change summed), with a separate kick envelope below 150 Hz and tempo by autocorrelation; beat accents decay as `exp(−age·k)·cos(age·w)`. The mapping from music to picture is storyboarded, not automatic. — [lintsinghua/paint-mv-skills](https://github.com/lintsinghua/paint-mv-skills)
- [C] Seeing Music (GPL-3.0, 115 stars): accessibility-led visualiser with spectrogram and Hilbert-scope modes for "the subtle textures of sound" and melody-path modes for "the paths and shapes of different melodies"; monophonic pitch tracking plus a polyphonic piano transcription mode. — [googlecreativelab/creatability-seeing-music](https://github.com/googlecreativelab/creatability-seeing-music)
- [C] Noise Garden (no licence stated): 900 particles in a Perlin flow field leaving fading trails; microphone level drives particle speed, noise scale ("field chaos") and trail brightness. — [NathanRullier/noise-garden](https://github.com/NathanRullier/noise-garden)
- [C] butterchurn (MIT), the WebGL port of a classic preset-based music visualiser, keeps the previous frame multiplied by a `decay` of 0.98 by default (0.9 in its blank preset). — [jberg/butterchurn src/visualizer.js](https://github.com/jberg/butterchurn)
- [C] Reusable building blocks with permissive licences: p5.brush (MIT, 1,430 stars) for brushes, fields and watercolour fills; ofxOilPaint (MIT) for bristle strokes; WebGL-Fluid-Simulation (MIT; defaults `DENSITY_DISSIPATION 1`, `VELOCITY_DISSIPATION 0.2`, `CURL 30`, `SPLAT_RADIUS 0.25`); spectral.js (MIT, 1,293 stars) for pigment-like mixing. — [p5.brush](https://github.com/acamposuribe/p5.brush); [ofxOilPaint](https://github.com/jagracar/ofxOilPaint); [WebGL-Fluid-Simulation](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation); [spectral.js](https://github.com/rvanwijnen/spectral.js)

### Inferences
- Judged by stars and by reading the code, the best-looking results come from projects that invest in the paint model (subtractive mixing, wet edges, paper grain, bristle or deformed-polygon strokes) and keep the audio mapping simple; the projects with richer audio analysis mostly render plain shapes. The opportunity is to combine both.
- Ideas worth taking: subtractive "absorption" accumulation so overlapping colours darken like pigment; golden-angle placement to avoid clustering; circle-of-fifths hue order if pitch class must be coloured; the two-pass halo-plus-core stroke as a cheap fallback; slow palette lock at high arousal; log-band spectral flux for onsets.
- Licence watch: GPL-3.0 (Seeing Music) and CC BY-NC-SA (Unstill-Life) code should not be copied into a differently licensed app; watercolor-audio and Noise Garden have no licence, so only their ideas are usable.

### Gaps
- Live demos could not be opened, so visual quality was judged from code, READMEs and stars only.
- GitHub code search (as opposed to repository search) needs authentication and was not available; projects that do not use the searched words in their name or description will have been missed.
- Star counts for some cloned repositories could not be re-queried through the API in this session and come from the search listing.

## 5. Generative-art techniques for organic variety (with parameters from open code)

### Takeaway
Hand-made-looking output in open code comes from a consistent recipe: one seeded random source, smooth (noise-correlated) variation at several scales instead of independent jitter, direction fields that strokes follow with inertia, many translucent layers, blue-noise placement, per-stroke variation of pressure and opacity, and colour scatter that preserves the intended average. Research on human timing supports correlated (1/f-like) variation over white noise.

### Cited Findings

Flow fields and steering
- [T] A flow-field curve needs a step length, a number of steps and a start position; the angle is looked up from a grid at each step; noise scale is the zoom of the field (small = broad sweeping currents, large = tight eddies). — [flow-fields essay](https://tylerxhobbs.com/words/flow-fields); [tutorial](https://benebsworth.pages.dev/lab/flow-field/)
- [C] color-wander (MIT): per seed, 50–2000 particles, 100–1000 steps, maximum radius 5–100 px, global alpha 0.5, round or square caps at 50/50, noise frequency up to 0.0002–0.004 per pixel. Per step: `angle = simplex3D(x·s, y·s, t)·2π`; `velocity += unit(angle)` then normalised (direction eases, giving inertia); speed = random 0.5–2 plus up to 2 from a luminance map; width = `radius · simplex(...) · (age/duration)`; lifetime random 1–500 steps then respawn; stroke colour picked from a shuffled 5-colour palette **including the background colour**, "so some strokes may seem to erase the other colours". — [mattdesl/color-wander config.js, lib/createRenderer.js](https://github.com/mattdesl/color-wander)
- [C] p5.brush ships named direction fields (hand, curved, zigzag, waves, seabed, spiral, columns) that bend every subsequent stroke, can be refreshed with a time argument so the field evolves, and a "hand" field giving a subtle wobble: `angle = 0.5·ba·sin(bs·r·c + rand(15..25))` with ba 5–10 degrees. — [p5.brush src/core/flowfield.js](https://github.com/acamposuribe/p5.brush)
- [C] Curl noise, `v = (∂n/∂y, −∂n/∂x)`, is divergence-free so particles "never clump into sinks"; evenly spaced streamlines stop a line when it comes within a separation distance of an existing one. — [algorithmic-art-skill references/techniques.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill); streamline library defaults `dSep 0.5`, `dTest 0.25`, `timeStep 0.01` (RK4) — [anvaka/streamlines](https://github.com/anvaka/streamlines)
- [C] A popular flow-field sketch (MIT, 86 stars) uses `angle = noise(x, y, z)·4π`, max speed 2, stroke weight 0.3 and line opacity 0.1, relying on accumulation of very faint lines. — [mkfreeman/flowFields](https://github.com/mkfreeman/flowFields)
- [C] A rule set for generative work bans the plain Perlin flow field as the sole idea because "every unguided model converges on" it, and asks instead for curl or domain-warped fields, evenly spaced streamlines, and weight and spacing modulated by an importance field. — [algorithmic-art-skill SKILL.md, techniques.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill)

Per-stroke variation
- [C] p5.brush randomises the pressure envelope of every stroke: pressure is a bell `1/(1 + |(pos − peak)/halfWidth|^(2c))` whose peak position, width and exponent are drawn per stroke (`a = rr(−1, 1)`, `b = rr(1, 1.5)`, `cp = rr(3, 3.5)`), wider before the peak than after (×1.2 vs ×0.8), so "repeated strokes feel less mechanical"; per-stroke alpha = `base·(1 + gaussian(0, noise))`. — [p5.brush src/stroke/stroke.js](https://github.com/acamposuribe/p5.brush)
- [C] Brush engine jitter controls and ranges: radius_by_random 0–1.5, offset_by_random 0–25 radii, tracking_noise 0–12, slow_tracking 0–10, per-dab hue/lightness/saturation shifts −2 to 2 that can be driven by speed, pressure, stroke progress or random. — [libmypaint brushsettings.json](https://github.com/mypaint/libmypaint)
- [C] Bristle-level variation: ±9% brightness per bristle, noise-steered path (`NOISE_FACTOR 0.007`), position averaged over 4 samples. — [ofxOilPaint](https://github.com/jagracar/ofxOilPaint)
- [C] "What hand-made means in code": glaze by overlap with 2–4 translucent passes per form, each inner pass shrunk and wobbled; bleed 0.1–0.4 and texture 0.35–0.95 with a darker border, varied within one picture; reserve light rather than painting white; contours broken to cover 40–85% of the outline, jittered and slightly off-register from the paint; speckle in Gaussian clouds; paper differing per picture. Avoid clean vector fills, perfect circles, identical repeated shapes, and "blur plus global noise as a substitute for watercolour". — [generative-painting-skills styles/watercolour/style-dna.md](https://github.com/debuu0007/generative-painting-skills)
- [C] "Motion written as code comes out mechanical, because code moves every part at once, on the same curve, by the same amount"; fixes are easing, avoiding identical twins, and offsetting timings, phases and seeds. — [ClaudeAnimationBase ANIMATION_GUIDE.md (MIT, 3,087 stars)](https://github.com/JohnHeibel/ClaudeAnimationBase)

Colour variation
- [C] Colour scatter that keeps the average true: each stroke takes the target colour C, picks one of up to four palette pigments in proportion to shares whose weighted mean equals C, and is painted as `C + k·(pigment − mean)`; the spread k is 0.10–0.35 depending on region and falls toward the darks "which stay quiet"; mixing is done in linear light. — [Unstill-Life architecture guide](https://github.com/RichardPieters/Unstill-Life)
- [C] Random darker border factor 0.15–0.7 and per-vertex bleed multipliers 0.85–1.4 on watercolour fills. — [p5.brush src/fill/fill.js](https://github.com/acamposuribe/p5.brush)

1/f versus white noise
- [R] Loudness fluctuations in music and speech and pitch fluctuations in music have 1/f spectra; melodies generated from white noise sounded "too random", from 1/f² noise "too correlated", and from 1/f noise the most pleasing. — [Voss & Clarke 1975, Nature 258:317](https://www.nature.com/articles/258317a0); [summary](https://gauss.math.yale.edu/fractals/Panorama/OneOverFMusic/VossMusic/VossMusic.html)
- [R] Human rhythmic timing errors are long-range correlated (power-law spectra with exponents 0.2–1.3) for laypeople and professionals, and listeners strongly preferred long-range-correlated "humanising" to the uncorrelated jitter used by audio software. — [Hennig et al. 2011, PLoS ONE 6:e26457](https://dx.doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0026457)
- [R] Some later measurements of sound spectra were steeper than 1/f, so the exact exponent is not universal. — [soundscape study](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/90187/1/Presence%20of%201f_whiterose.pdf)
- [T] Pink noise can be made by summing several random sequences refreshed at octave-spaced rates (one sequence updated per step in the staggered variant). — [DSPRelated article](https://www.dsprelated.com/showarticle/908.php)

Placement
- [R] Uniform random sampling clusters and jittered grids reduce but do not eliminate clustering; Poisson-disc sampling enforces a minimum spacing and can be generated in O(N). — [Bridson 2007](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/bridson-siggraph07-poissondisk.pdf)
- [C] A JavaScript Poisson-disc library (MIT) takes minDistance, maxDistance (default 2× min) and tries (default 30). — [kchapelier/poisson-disk-sampling](https://github.com/kchapelier/poisson-disk-sampling)
- [C] Lattice placement with jitter, where every stroke property is a pure function of its cell's integer coordinates, plus a second pass shifted half a cell with its own randomness and 0.75 of cells firing; strokes are enabled by comparing a fixed per-cell number with a threshold, so adding strokes never reshuffles existing ones. — [Unstill-Life architecture guide](https://github.com/RichardPieters/Unstill-Life)
- [C] Golden-angle (137.5 degree) spiral placement for successive blooms. — [watercolor-audio js/main.js](https://github.com/komei222/watercolor-audio)

Layered translucent passes
- [T] Layered watercolour recipe: draw a deformed polygon at about 4% opacity and repeat for 30–100 layers, each layer starting from a shared base polygon deformed 4–5 more times, with a different texture mask per layer; a talk write-up of the same method used 5% opacity and 50 layers. — [watercolour essay](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/a-guide-to-simulating-watercolor-paint-with-generative-art); [talk write-up](https://opensource.com/article/17/9/how-hack-painting)
- [C] A Processing implementation (MIT, 56 stars) builds 100 layers per blob, deforming the base polygon recursively to depth 5 with variance random(r/10, r/4), and **interleaves** 5 layers at a time across blobs so colours mix in the overlaps instead of the last blob covering the rest. — [sighack/watercolor-simulation watercolor.pde](https://github.com/sighack/watercolor-simulation)

Seeding and reproducibility
- [C] mulberry32 generator seeded from an FNV-style string hash, with `fork(salt)` to make independent sub-streams and a coordinate hash `n2(x, y)` for order-independent values. — [generative-painting-skills engine/src/rng.js](https://github.com/debuu0007/generative-painting-skills)
- [C] "All randomness flows from the seed. Same seed + params = identical output. Never call Math.random() or read the clock in the art path"; judge a system on a contact sheet of at least 9 seeds and aim for at least 7 good; fix bad seeds with rejection tests or narrower ranges, not by hunting lucky seeds. — [algorithmic-art-skill SKILL.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill)
- [C] Per-object `hash(i)` for stable values; per-element reseeding by a stable key so that one moving element does not shift the random stream of everything drawn after it; line "boil" by reseeding jitter 12 times a second. — [ClaudeAnimationBase ANIMATION_GUIDE.md](https://github.com/JohnHeibel/ClaudeAnimationBase); [paint-mv-skills techniques.md](https://github.com/lintsinghua/paint-mv-skills)
- [C] A utility library (MIT) offers seeded value, range, gaussian, 1D–4D simplex noise with frequency and amplitude, points on or inside a circle, and weighted choice from a set. — [canvas-sketch-util docs/random.md](https://github.com/mattdesl/canvas-sketch-util)

### Inferences
- Three nested time scales give "one hand, no two marks alike": (1) per-painting constants drawn once from the seed (brush family, palette, field character, how wet the painter works); (2) slow drifts over sections, generated as low-frequency noise or 1/f sequences (dominant stroke angle, load habits, hue bias); (3) per-stroke deviations drawn from narrow Gaussians around the current drift. White noise should be reserved for the finest level (bristle streaks, grain).
- Replace every independent `random()` on width, pressure, hue and value with a correlated source: either smooth noise sampled along stroke index or time, or a sum of 4–6 octave-spaced random sequences (pink). The timing and melody studies suggest listeners and viewers read correlated variation as human and white jitter as machine noise; this transfer from timing to brush variation is my assumption, not a tested result.
- Starting ranges taken from the code above: per-stroke opacity ±10–20% Gaussian; per-bristle brightness ±9%; pressure peak position and sharpness re-drawn per stroke; colour scatter k = 0.1–0.35 around the target with a zero-mean constraint and less scatter in darks; dab position jitter of a fraction of a radius; contour or stroke coverage deliberately incomplete (40–85%).
- Direction field for a music painting: a base field (curl noise or a domain-warped field) whose character is set per seed, slowly rotated or re-seeded at section boundaries, with strokes following it with inertia; faster, louder passages increase turbulence (higher noise frequency), as two of the projects do with microphone level.
- Tool choice "with habit": no open painting code using a Markov chain for tool or gesture selection was found (a repository search returned nothing). A first-order chain with a high self-transition probability, biased by the current mood so that the stationary distribution matches the music, would give runs of one tool with occasional switches. The transition numbers are untested; something like 0.7–0.85 probability of keeping the current tool is a starting guess.
- Seed design: seed = hash(audio fingerprint or file bytes) combined with a user-visible run number. The same file and run number then reproduces the painting; changing the run number gives a different painting of the same music. Fork separate streams for placement, colour, pressure and field so that a change in one mapping does not reshuffle the others, and derive each mark's randomness from a hash of its note identity (onset time and frequency bin), not from draw order.

### Gaps
- The flow-field and watercolour essays could not be opened; specific recommended numbers for grid resolution and step length could not be confirmed and are not quoted.
- The candidate count usually quoted for Poisson-disc sampling was confirmed only from a library default (30 tries), not from the paper.
- No research was found on whether 1/f-correlated variation in stroke width, pressure or hue is perceived as more natural; only the timing and melody studies exist.
- No open implementation of Markov-chain tool selection for painting was found.

## 6. Composition over a whole song on one canvas: avoiding mud and over-filling, fading, and laying out time

### Takeaway
Open projects that accumulate paint in real time avoid overload by exponential fading with half-lives around a second, which suits visualisers but would erase a song-length painting. For a persistent canvas, the usable rules from painting teaching and generative-art code are: plan value before hue, cap coverage and reserve bare ground, limit how many colours can mix in one place, let layers dry before adding more, and compress old paint toward the ground instead of deleting it.

### Cited Findings
- [T] Mud comes from overmixing ("mix just enough to hit the hue, then let the brush do the blending on the canvas"), from more than three or at most four pigments in a mixture, and from darkening with black; one teaching view is that "muddy" colours are simply mixtures of the wrong relative temperature for where they sit; layer "fat over lean". — [Realism Today](https://realismtoday.com/oil-painting-lesson-10-ways-to-avoid-muddy-color-mixing/); [Draw Paint Academy](https://drawpaintacademy.com/muddy-colors/); [painting blog on the mud debate](https://gurneyjourney.blogspot.ch/2008/05/mud-debate.html)
- [C] "Alpha accumulation is a palette decision: thousands of overlapping translucent strokes shift hue and value where they pile up; design for the accumulated colour"; never interpolate raw RGB because "midpoints go gray and muddy"; 3–5 colours at roughly 60% ground, 30% secondary, 10% accent; accent under about 15% of pixels; more than about 5 distinct hues means tighten. — [algorithmic-art-skill references/palettes.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill)
- [C] Composition rules: one "importance field" I(x, y) modulating density, weight, contrast and scale together; three passes (quiet large-scale ground, mid-contrast structure, small high-contrast detail concentrated where importance is high); at least two scales differing by 5× or more; "reserve 20–40% of the canvas as near-empty ground"; for dense accumulation, accumulate counts in floats and tone-map with `lum = (log1p(h)/log1p(max))^γ` instead of clamped alpha; "uniform texture, nowhere to look" is the typical failure. — [algorithmic-art-skill references/composition.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill)
- [C] "One palette family per plate: 3–6 hues plus the ground"; "negative space is designed, not left over"; alternate dense and sparse, dark and pale. — [generative-painting-skills style-dna.md](https://github.com/debuu0007/generative-painting-skills)
- [C] An automatic oil painter rejects strokes by coverage rules: stop adding to a region once the painted fraction along a stroke exceeds 0.65; reject a stroke that would destroy more than 0.4 of already well-painted pixels; work from large brushes to small, dividing size by 1.3 per pass down to a minimum of 4. — [ofxOilPaint src/ofxOilSimulator.cpp](https://github.com/jagracar/ofxOilPaint)
- [C] Fading constants in use: dye multiplied by 0.992 per step in a watercolour audio visualiser ("pigment lingers like a stain"); previous frame multiplied by 0.98 in a preset music visualiser (0.9 in its blank preset); a fluid simulation default density dissipation of 1 (adjustable 0–4). — [watercolor-audio js/fluid.js](https://github.com/komei222/watercolor-audio); [butterchurn](https://github.com/jberg/butterchurn); [WebGL-Fluid-Simulation script.js](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation)
- [C] Wet/dry as separate states: fills that touch a still-wet fill feather into it, and "the paper dries" at each render; other engines keep a dual wet/dry layer with a settable drying rate, evaporation, deposition and re-wetting. — [p5.brush README](https://github.com/acamposuribe/p5.brush); [efx-physic-paint](https://github.com/electroheadfx/efx-physic-paint); [wetpaint](https://github.com/bgyss/wetpaint)
- [C] Pigment-like mixing has a cost for light effects: "a yellow glow painted over blue turns green, and a thin wash over it turns grey", so light is added in a separate additive pass under the paper grain. — [ClaudeAnimationBase ANIMATION_GUIDE.md](https://github.com/JohnHeibel/ClaudeAnimationBase)
- [C] Pacing the arrival of marks: an "even pace" control (0.6 by default) converts bursty birth times into a steadier number of marks per second while keeping their order. — [Unstill-Life architecture guide](https://github.com/RichardPieters/Unstill-Life)
- [C] Layouts used by audio painters: fixed ring with one anchor per pitch class; golden-angle spiral scatter; automatic alternation between layouts every 15 s. — [watercolor-audio js/main.js](https://github.com/komei222/watercolor-audio)
- [R] People who read left-to-right place time on a left-to-right mental line, and the direction tracks the writing direction of the dominant language (right-to-left readers reverse it); 3-year-olds choose at random; mirror-reading practice can reverse it. — [Stanford thesis record](https://purl.stanford.edu/cz142gh0899); [Pitt & Casasanto](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/344/files/Pitt_uchicago_0330D_14349.pdf); [Mental timeline overview](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_timeline)

### Inferences
My calculations from the constants above: a per-frame multiplier of 0.992 at 60 frames per second halves paint in about 1.4 s; 0.98 in about 0.6 s; 0.9 in about 0.1 s. Thirty layers at 4% opacity reach about 71% coverage, 50 layers about 87%, 100 layers about 98%; at 5%, 50 layers reach about 92%.

- Those half-lives are visualiser values. A painting that should still show the first verse at the end of a four-minute piece needs either no decay or a half-life measured in minutes, so fading must be redesigned and not just slowed.
- Fading options and trade-offs:
  - Plain exponential fade toward the ground: simplest and smooth, but uniform; old passages vanish and the result is a moving trail, not a finished painting.
  - Exponential fade to a floor (for example down to 30–50% of original contrast, never to zero): old paint "sinks back" into an underpainting and stays legible as history. This is the closest analogue of letting a layer dry and go quiet.
  - Per-layer (per-section) fading: each musical section paints on its own layer; when a new section starts, earlier layers are lowered in saturation and contrast by one step. Gives visible structure and matches the "dry, then glaze" idea, at the cost of memory.
  - "Dry then glaze": paint is wet for a few seconds (can blend, bloom and be picked up), then dries and becomes fixed; later paint over a dry area glazes or scumbles optically instead of mixing physically. This prevents the pigment-mixing mud that comes from endlessly re-stirring wet paint, and it is how the teaching sources say to avoid it.
- Opacity and coverage budget: track per-pixel coverage and thickness. As local coverage approaches a cap (the oil painter's 0.65 is a tested starting value), make new marks there thinner, smaller, or scumbled (broken, letting the layer below show), or shift them to glazes. Keep a global target of 20–40% of the canvas at or near bare ground and stop or thin quiet-passage marks first.
- Value and saturation management: keep most of the picture in a mid-value band and reserve the darkest and most saturated paint for the loudest or most salient events, which also matches loudness-to-salience. Desaturate and value-compress old layers before new saturated paint lands on them, so accents stay scarce (under about 15% of pixels).
- Limit simultaneous colour: at most 3–4 pigments may physically mix in any wet region; hold one palette family per section; mix in a pigment or perceptual space, never raw RGB.
- Pace: use an even-pace transform on onsets so dense passages do not dump all their paint in one place at once; density can still rise, but with a ceiling per unit area and time.
- Time layout with height fixed to frequency:

| Layout | How it works | Readability | Painting quality | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Left to right, whole song across the width | x = time | highest for left-to-right readers; matches scores and spectrograms; structure (repeats, build-ups) visible at a glance | reads as a frieze or chart; each moment gets a thin column | long songs squeeze marks; little overlap, so little painterly mixing |
| Wrapping rows | several left-to-right passes stacked, each row its own frequency axis | good, like lines of text or staves | repeated bands can look like wallpaper | frequency resolution divided by the number of rows; breaks "height is frequency" for the canvas as a whole |
| Repeated left-to-right passes over the same canvas (palimpsest) | x = time within a section or bar group; each section paints over the previous, now dried and sunk back | medium: recurring material lands in the same place and reinforces itself | strongest layering, glazing and depth | needs the fading and budget rules above or it fills up |
| Radial | angle = time, radius = frequency | medium; cyclic forms read well | strong central composition | conflicts with a vertical frequency axis unless radius replaces height |
| Free drifting | a slowly wandering brush position (noise or golden-angle steps) sets x; y stays frequency | lowest for time; pitch and timbre still readable | most painterly and varied | time order is lost; needs importance-field control to avoid even scatter |

- The direction of a time axis is a cultural convention (it follows reading direction), so left-to-right should be a default that can be mirrored, not a fixed rule.

### Gaps
- No source describes opacity budgeting or long-duration accumulation for music paintings specifically; the options above are assembled from adjacent practice and are untested.
- Nothing authoritative was retrieved on oil paint "sinking in" and oiling out, or on drying times, so the "sink back" behaviour is an analogy without numbers.
- No study was found comparing time layouts for readability of music visualisations.

## 7. Colour: automatic harmonious palettes, variation, and pitch class or key to hue

### Takeaway
Build palettes in a perceptual space (OKLab or OKLCH), from a few anchors chosen by mood, with lightness structure decided first and one scarce accent. Listeners do not share a pitch-class-to-hue code, and historical schemes contradict one another, so a key or pitch-class colour can only be an optional stylistic tint, best ordered by the circle of fifths.

### Cited Findings
- [C] Interpolating raw RGB greys the midpoints; interpolate in OKLab/OKLCH or hand-pick stops; drop-in sRGB/OKLab conversion code and a multi-anchor ramp builder are published under MIT; starter palettes list the ground first; pure black or white grounds are "almost always better slightly lifted/tinted". — [algorithmic-art-skill references/palettes.md](https://github.com/Pardesco/algorithmic-art-skill)
- [T] OKLCH keeps midpoints saturated and perceived brightness even because lightness, chroma and hue are interpolated separately, but hue is undefined at zero chroma, so paths through or near grey misbehave; OKLab's straight path is safer when endpoints are far apart in hue or one is near-neutral; generated ramps can leave the display gamut at high lightness and need gamut mapping; one framework switched its gradient default from OKLCH to OKLab for this reason. — [preprint arXiv 2606.15352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15352); [Hacker News discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45010876); [conference talk notes](https://conffab.com/presentation/what-the-color/) (practitioner sources)
- [C] A ramp generator (MIT) parameterises a palette as: number of colours (default 9), start hue (random 0–360), hue cycles (1 = full wheel, 0.5 = 180 degrees), where the start hue sits in the ramp (0.5), saturation range [0.4, 0.35] eased by x², lightness range [random·0.1, 0.9] eased by x^1.5; a helper returns unique random hues with a minimum separation (90 degrees, capped at 360/count). — [meodai/rampensau README](https://github.com/meodai/rampensau)
- [C] Another generator (MIT) makes palettes by drawing lines between two or more anchor colours in a polar hue space and sampling points along them (4 per anchor pair by default) with per-axis easing; a curated set (MIT) stores each palette as colours plus a stroke colour and a background colour; one painter picks a random 5-colour palette, shuffles it and uses the first colour as ground. — [meodai/poline](https://github.com/meodai/poline); [kgolid/chromotome](https://github.com/kgolid/chromotome); [mattdesl/color-wander](https://github.com/mattdesl/color-wander)
- [C] Fourteen worked palette families, each a ground plus 3–6 key colours, spread across dark, pale and saturated grounds, with the rule "saturated plates need pale neighbours". — [generative-painting-skills style-dna.md](https://github.com/debuu0007/generative-painting-skills)
- [R] Mood hue anchors from the music–colour studies: fast/major = lighter, more saturated, yellower; slow/minor = darker, greyer, bluer; happy = yellow; anger = red (and large); sad = dark blue; loud and distorted = darker, redder, more saturated. — [Palmer et al. 2013](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1212562110); [Lindborg & Friberg 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4671663/); [Whiteford et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6240980)
- [R] Among pitch-class–colour synaesthetes the average colour gradient across the twelve pitch classes resembled a rainbow; a follow-up added 18 synaesthetes without strong absolute pitch; a 2024 retest found the rainbow pattern mainly in "two-step" synaesthetes while "one-step" synaesthetes had individual colours. — [Front Psychol 2024, 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1482714](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1482714/full)
- [R] For non-synaesthetes the documented regularity is pitch height to lightness (high = lighter), not pitch class to hue. — [Chromesthesia overview](https://Wikipedia.com/wiki/Chromesthesia); [Anikin & Johansson 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6407832/)
- [T] Historical pitch-to-colour scales disagree: an 18th-century keyboard scheme put blue on the tonic, green on the second, yellow on the third and red on the fifth; a later critic proposed a different organisation; a late-19th-century colour-organ patent laid the spectrum along the octave by frequency ratio; several 20th-century scales followed the spectrum-to-scale analogy from an early optics treatise; pitch to hue is nonetheless "the most often proposed mapping", and critics note the two dimensions are fundamentally different. — [colour-scales comparison](https://rhythmiclight.com/1998/archives/ideas/colorscales.html); [hue-to-pitch page](https://rhythmiclight.com/correspondences/hue-to-pitch/); [colour-music history](https://lukemckernan.com/2014/08/21/colour-music/)
- [C] Two open mappings in current code: pitch class to hue in circle-of-fifths order, so related notes are neighbours; and pitch class to a chromatic rainbow with octave as lightness. A third uses the key only as a tint of at most ±0.06 of the hue wheel on top of a mood colour, inverted for minor. — [watercolor-audio](https://github.com/komei222/watercolor-audio); [musicolors](https://github.com/ChungHaLee/musicolors); [music-reactive-lighting](https://github.com/CanYuzbey/music-reactive-lighting)

### Inferences
- Palette recipe: (1) choose ground value from valence and arousal (light, warm-tinted ground for bright moods; dark, cool-tinted for sombre or aggressive ones; never pure white or black); (2) choose a base hue from mood (yellow–orange for happy and energetic, red for angry, blue for sad, blue-green to green for calm, following the studies and the open two-arc mapping); (3) generate 3–5 colours around it with a restricted hue sweep, about 30–60 degrees for calm or unified moods and up to about 120–180 degrees for energetic ones, with chroma scaled by arousal; (4) add one accent near the complement, kept scarce; (5) interpolate and mix in OKLab, with gamut mapping. The hue-sweep widths are my proposal.
- Variation between runs without losing fidelity: let the seed rotate the base hue by a small amount (about ±15–20 degrees), choose which palette member is the ground, and choose the accent side, while the mood fixes lightness and chroma, the dimensions for which the evidence is strongest.
- Pitch class or key: since the canvas already encodes pitch by height (plus lightness and size), hue need not encode pitch at all. If a harmonic colour is wanted, use circle-of-fifths ordering for the key or chord root, applied as a small rotation of the mood palette (tens of degrees at most), so modulations produce neighbouring shifts and distant keys produce visible contrast. A chromatic rainbow puts semitone neighbours, which clash harmonically, in adjacent hues and gives no perceptual benefit to non-synaesthetes.
- Per-note colour inside the palette: pick the palette member by timbre or source (for example spectral-centroid band), then shift lightness up with pitch and chroma up with loudness, and scatter with the zero-mean rule from section 5 so the average stays on target.

### Gaps
- The original synaesthesia papers and any study testing pitch-class–colour agreement in non-synaesthetes were not retrieved; "no shared code" rests on the absence of such evidence in the sources found plus the hue results in section 1.
- OKLCH guidance comes from practitioner pages and one preprint, not from a colour-science standard.
- No research was found on which automatically generated palettes people judge to fit a given piece of music, beyond the emotion-alignment dataset paper, whose results tables were not accessible.
