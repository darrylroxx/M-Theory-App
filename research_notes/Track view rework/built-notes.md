## Reverb on converted tracks
Decision: a converted song keeps the reverb it was heard with. `toTimeline` sets it; nothing in `src/audio/` changed.

What the old engine did (read in `rack.ts`, `synth.ts`, `mix.ts`; measured on the pretend audio graph with `sendLog`, and as real sound in a browser):
- Every way a row is played gets a voice (`slotOf`: track, tone as JSON, sound as JSON). All voices of a row send through the row's one `sendFader`, and each voice joins that fader to its own room once more. With N voices on one room every note of the row reached the room N times: N times the reverb the knob says. With voices on different rooms, every note also went to the other voices' rooms.
- The count is of voices, not of tracks after conversion. Two things make more voices than tracks: the first chords get a voice in every section, silent ones too (a section with no chords and no instrument chosen is a second voice, on the strings); and a tone that only remembers another instrument's settings is a different voice (fixture 3 has four voices and three tracks). So a row that is NOT split can have had twice the reverb (fixture 10's chords). A row is left exactly as before only when the old engine ran one voice for it.
- Live, a voice exists from the first time its section plays, so the old reverb grew during the first pass and then stayed. The WAV had all of it from the first note. The WAV is the reference.

What the conversion does (`keepReverb`, `reverbTimes`, `voicesOf` in `migrate5.ts`):
- Reverb: up to where one voice sends `times` as much, by the knob's own curve (`verbSendOf`, a power of 1.3: `space × times^(1/1.3)`), stored to four decimals.
- Level: more Reverb turns the sound's own mix down (`rack.ts`: −7 dB × space^1.6, on the dry sound and on the send alike), so the formula on its own would leave the track 1.4 dB quieter at two voices and 4.6 dB at four. Level comes up by as much. What Level cannot give (it ends at 1) goes on the track's fader in tenths of a dB (fixture 3: +0.8 dB on each of its three chords tracks).
- Shimmer (strings only): it reaches the room past the Reverb knob, so it is multiplied by `times`.
- Different rooms: one voice cannot send to the other rooms. The track's own room gets as much as all rooms had together, added as power, a room of a kind that returns less counting for less (`times = √(Σ (voices in room × return of that kind)²)`). Kept: the track's own sound and how loud its reverb is. Not kept: which room it rings in.
- Not kept, and why: a Reverb that would pass 1 stays at 1 (room for 4.78 times from the starting 0.3; at 0.9 with four voices 29 % of the reverb is left); behind a synth's arpeggio or pulse the track's Reverb counts only up to 0.15 (`rackForPlay`), so a track at 0.15 or more is left alone and keeps 1/N (fixture 9's arpeggio: half); a fader already near +6 dB.

Rendered level, converted against old (a browser's offline render, the WAV as `wav5` writes it): fixture 3 was 0.485, is 1.000; fixture 15 was 0.932, is 1.000; fixtures 9, 10 and 18 were 0.956, 0.957 and 0.975, are 0.996, 1.000 and 1.000. Fixture 8 has no row on more than one voice: second for second it is the same sound before and after. Its file is one bar longer (a recording whose bar is no longer in its section is kept as a silent clip at beat 36, past the old end at 40, and `flatten` plays to 44), which is what lowered its average level by 4 to 5 %. That is still so.

For later steps:
- `renderlog.ts` has `sendLog(items, bpm, rename?, mix?)`: for every note, the gain to the speakers and to each room. `reverb.test.ts` holds old against converted for all 18 fixtures, and holds the thirteen fixtures with no such row to the very result of before (a hash).
- `equal.test.ts` compares each converted voice's whole sound with the old one's as before; for the five fixtures with such a row the expected Reverb and Level are written out there (`MORE_REVERB`).
- An idea converted by an earlier build of this branch keeps the lower reverb (conversion runs once). Restoring its "(before track view)" entry converts it again. Such an idea also no longer equals a fresh conversion of its original, so `arrive` treats it as edited: a newer old copy lands beside it.

- **Known and not fixed.** (1) After "Move song" a harmony clip may show the "source changed" dot (`transpose` does not rewrite the marks). (2) The old engine sends a track's reverb once for every sound slot the track has (`rack.ts`: each rack connects the track's shared send to its room), so a track that sounded N ways across sections had N times the reverb; converted, each of its N tracks has one. Measured as WAV level, old against new: 15 of 18 fixtures within 2 %, fixtures 8 and 15 about 4 to 5 % lower, fixture 3 (one chords track on four sounds, nothing else playing) 41 % lower. Not changed here: it needs a decision (keep what the knobs say, or raise Reverb on split tracks in the conversion).

- **`addRecording5`'s `o.nudge` is milliseconds** (the studio setting); the takes store frames. Its ids come in this order: the recording, each take, the clip.
- **The nudge.** `renudge5(song, ms, was?)`: a take that does not say its nudge stays unless `was` is given (the host gives the old value when the setting changes). `stampTakes5(song, ms)` marks such takes as cut with `ms` without moving them. `afterRestore(song, nudge)` now takes the setting and moves the takes (undo, redo); `onScreen(song, nudge)` does that and marks, for an idea opened and, once, for the song the app starts with. So the song on screen never holds a take that does not say its nudge, the undo steps made from it do not either, and an undo after a nudge change moves rightly (while an undo that moves nothing still gives the very same song object back). When the setting changes, the host also marks such takes in every saved idea with the old value, so they move when that idea opens. **`fingerprint5` no longer reads a take's `start` and `nudge`** (`library5.ts`): the nudge is a setting of the device, and without this an idea would read as changed, and ask to be saved, just for being opened under another nudge.
- **Extras in `audioclips.ts`**: `useShelved5` (the take's whole recording comes off the shelf onto the track), `emptyShelf5`, `renudgeTakes5`, `stampTakes5`. `useTake5` on a take with `at === null` sets `at` on every unplaced take of its recording.
- **`Shelved.saves` is still never counted, on purpose**: a shelf entry only goes by Empty (or by Use). Dropping it after two saves would lose a recording without the user asking.
- **Undo.** Every take-list edit is one `edit` step ("Use take", "Keep take", "Delete take", "Remove recording", "Use removed recording", "Empty removed recordings", "Apply sound", "Vocal sound" folded per track, "Input", "Record"). The 20-second ↶ on delete, remove and Empty is offered only while the song is as that edit left it. Because `keepTakes` runs on redo too, redoing a deletion brings the take back unplaced instead of deleting it again.
- **Keys.** K acts while the record window has the focus, and, while a recording runs, whenever no other window has it. Ctrl+R with the focus inside a window, the take list or a box is taken by a listener in `Host` (the key table leaves it alone there, and the desktop window would reload the page).
- **While a recording or a rehearsal runs** the host's player does not start (`recBusy`): Space, ▶ and auditions do nothing.
- **Monitor from the top bar.** Choosing Effects while the interface is not open opens the record window (the page lists and opens the interface only with its window open, as before).
- **Inputs.** `inputs.count` is `studio.inputs` (stored by the page whenever the interface opens), at most 2, 2 until it has been open. The chosen device's channel count is not read by the host.
- **Seen with a stand-in engine, not changed (the old page's own path, the same before this step):** when the page closes and opens the interface in one go (the Buffer choice changed, another device picked, a driver reset), its close is sent after the open that follows it: the cleanup waits for `comeBack()` before `engineClose()`, while `engineOpen()` is called at once. The stand-in, which like the real engine closes whatever is open on `engine_close`, ended closed while the page showed it open (dev build and production build alike); closing and opening the record window put it right. A recording going on through a reset was kept either way. To be checked on real hardware; the fix would be to let the open wait for the close that was asked for first.
- **Also in the take list:** `pick` (the take of the clip selected on the lane is the one selected in the list). In a browser the page is mounted only while its window is open (there is no interface to keep).

The close-after-open order (what was found):
- `desktop.ts` sends open, close, start and stop in the order its functions are **called**, and `src-tauri/src/engine.rs` does what each says: `open()` closes whatever is open and opens anew; `engine_close` closes whatever is open (`close_engine()` takes `LIVE`). The old page's cleanup called `comeBack().then(() => engineClose())` and the effect that followed called `engineOpen()` at once, so the open was called first whenever a close and an open came in one go: a Buffer change with the interface open, another device, a driver reset (and, in a development build, the very first open). The new interface was then closed by the old one's close, while the page showed it open: no meters, "The interface is not open" on Record until the window was closed and opened again. A recording going on through a reset was kept either way (the open puts it by, the close hands it back).
- Now: close, then open, in every case (`engine.test.ts` holds the order against a pretend desktop side that behaves as the Rust does, and shows the old order failing on it). After a reset in the middle of a recording the calls are `engine_close` (hands the recording back), `fx_render`, `engine_open`: the vocal sound is put on the salvaged recording while the interface opens, as before, but the open no longer comes first.

- The last render of the backing is still not kept between recordings (spec F.3 step 2; step 1d left it to this step). It is not in this step's brief, and a stale backing under a recording is the one mistake this path must never make: every recording renders afresh, as before.
- The record window keeps one meter (the track's own input) and the input chooser; every input's meter is in Settings → Audio. Inputs are still 1 and 2 (step 6).

Rough edges left:
- Two sound windows opened one after the other stand in the same place (the windows' own first place), the second over the first.
- The sheet's toast after a keep is offered only while the song is exactly as the keep left it.
- More than eight dry takes on bypassed tracks at once are all kept (they are named to the take cache with the song's own), but they are read only when the bypass goes on, so the first moments after Alt + click a clip of such a track is silent until its file is in.
